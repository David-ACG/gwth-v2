# Gender pay gap, 5 April 2026 snapshot — note for the board

## Do we have to publish?

Yes. We had **262 relevant employees** on 5 April 2026, and the duty to publish applies at **250 or more**. The test is a headcount of people on our own contracts — including part-time staff and people on maternity, sick or other leave. It is **not** based on full-time equivalents, so our roughly 210 FTE does not take us below the threshold, and the view that we need not publish is mistaken. Agency workers are excluded (their agencies count them); even without them we are above 250.

## The figures

Hourly pay covers **248 "full-pay relevant" employees** — the 262 minus 14 paid less than usual in the April 2026 payroll because of maternity, sickness or unpaid leave.

- **Mean hourly pay:** men £19.37, women £15.50 — a **mean gender pay gap of 20.0%**.
- **Median gap: 5.0%.** The middle-paid man earns about 5% more per hour than the middle-paid woman.
- The mean is far above the median because a few senior roles, mostly held by men, are paid well above the typical rate.

**Bonuses** (12 months to 5 April 2026): 34.2% of men and 28.4% of women received bonus pay. The mean bonus gap is 78.1% and the median 73.5%, driven by large annual bonuses to a few senior men (mean bonus: men £3,857, women £846).

**Pay quartiles** (women's share, lowest paid to highest): lower 77.4%, lower middle 71.0%, upper middle 74.2%, upper 53.2% — see `chart.svg`. Women are the majority in every quartile, but their share falls sharply at the top. That, plus who gets large bonuses, drives the gaps.

## Corrections and limitations

- The payroll export had **15 exact duplicate lines** (same line ID twice). We removed them; otherwise April pay for 12 people and bonuses for 3 would be overstated.
- A November bonus later reversed as paid in error nets to zero, so that person counts as receiving no bonus.
- We excluded 14 agency workers, 5 people who started after 5 April 2026, and 18 who left before it.
- **Ethnicity pay gap: not available.** The HR system holds no ethnicity data, so `answers.json` shows `null`. To report it in future we must collect voluntary, self-reported ethnicity data.

Figures follow our payroll provider's method note. No individual is identifiable from these outputs.
