# Four-day week: pilot plan

## Does the proposal work as written?

**No, not for the showroom.**

- **Saturdays:** Patterns A and B put nobody on a Saturday. Cover would be 0 against a minimum of 6 (1 key holder, 2 designers).
- **Overtime can't fill the gap:** only four showroom staff have volunteered (S02, S07, S09, S11; F03 is a fitter). All four would give 4 staff and 1 designer, at £617.40 a Saturday (£7,408.80 over the pilot), and those four would be back on five-day weeks.
- **Survey:** the 87% is 13 "Yes" answers out of 15 responses. That's 32.5% of staff. The link could be forwarded, and few of the showroom (3 of 12) or fitters (3 of 14) answered. 11 of 15 want Fridays off, which a half-and-half split can't give.
- **Arithmetic:** hours fall 14.67% (1,500 to 1,280 a week) on the same pay, so each hour must be about 17% more productive. Days get longer (8 hours, not 7.5); one survey comment asked that fitters' days not get longer.

## Your realistic options

1. **As written:** Saturday cover falls short every week.
2. **As written, plus the showroom rota below:** no routine overtime.
3. **Office and warehouse first, showroom and fitting later:** lower risk, but may feel unfair.
4. **Delay:** run a proper survey and consult each team first.

## A pilot you could run: 4 January to 28 March 2027

Other teams: Patterns A and B as proposed, once team leads confirm cover (the warehouse has one key holder).

Showroom: four patterns of four consecutive opening days:

- **A, Mon–Thu:** S02, S05, S06, S09
- **B, Tue–Fri:** S07, S11
- **C, Wed–Sat:** S01, S03, S12
- **D, Fri–Tue:** S04, S08, S10

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Staff (minimum) | 7 (5) | 9 (5) | 9 (5) | 9 (5) | 8 (5) | 6 (6) |
| Key holders | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Designers | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 |

- **Weekdays:** at least 2 people spare.
- **Saturdays:** no one spare, as now.
  - Leave: someone off Saturdays works the shift and takes a weekday off that week.
  - Sickness: an overtime volunteer covers it (£138.60–£176.40); that's why all four volunteers are off Saturdays.
- **Trade-off:** six people work every Saturday (now about six in ten). Check they agree, and that S05's and S06's childcare still fits. Same-role swaps keep these figures.
- **Decide before you start:**
  - A Saturday week is 31 paid hours, not 32 (as now, 37 not 37.5).
  - Good Friday (26 March) falls in the pilot: agree a bank-holiday rule so Friday-off staff don't lose out.

## Measuring success

Set targets before January; compare with January–March 2026. Figures are suggestions.

- **Cover:** no day below minimum, and Saturday overtime only for absences.
- **Business:** design appointments, orders and sales within 5% of last year. Fitting jobs on schedule, and no rise in complaints.
- **People:** sickness absence, plus an all-staff survey run by HR in December, week 6 and week 12.
- **Recruitment:** applications per fitter or designer vacancy.
