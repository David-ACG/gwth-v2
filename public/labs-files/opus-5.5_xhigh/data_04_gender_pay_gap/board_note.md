# Gender pay gap: 5 April 2026 snapshot

## Must we publish?

**Yes.** The threshold is 250 relevant employees, counted as people, not full-time equivalents. On 5 April 2026 we employed 262 people (183 women, 79 men). Our FTE is about 210, hence the chair's figure, but 166 staff work part-time and each counts as one person. Agency workers are counted by their agencies. We must publish on the government service and our website by **4 April 2027**, with a statement signed by a director.

## Figures

- Mean hourly pay: men £19.37, women £15.50
- Gender pay gap: mean 20.0%, median 5.0%
- Bonus gap: mean 78.1%, median 73.5%
- Received a bonus: men 34.2%, women 28.4%
- Women in each pay quartile, lowest to highest: 77.4%, 71.0%, 74.2%, 53.2% (see chart)

## What they mean

Women are 70% of our staff but only 53% of the best-paid quarter. The median gap is small because most roles in our homes pay similar rates. The mean gap is much larger because all five directors, our highest earners, are men. Without them it would be about 7.5%. They also received about £72,000 of the £104,000 in bonuses paid to men. A pay gap is not unequal pay for equal work; it reflects who holds which jobs.

## What we corrected

- Sex was recorded in eight formats (F, Woman, male and others); we standardised them.
- 15 payroll lines appeared twice (12 in April 2026 pay, 3 March 2026 bonuses). We counted each once.
- A November 2025 bonus was reversed in December, so it counts as nothing.
- We excluded 18 people who left before 5 April and 5 who started after.
- For 26 people with later hours changes agreed, we used hours in force on 5 April.
- 14 people (12 women, 2 men) were on reduced pay in April because of maternity, sick or unpaid leave. As the rules require, they are excluded from hourly pay and quartiles but included in bonus figures.
- Only bonuses paid 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 count, so March 2025 bonuses and long-service awards paid 4 April 2025 are excluded.

## What we could not work out

**Ethnicity pay gap.** Our HR records contain no ethnicity information. Guessing it from names would be unreliable and inappropriate. Reporting it would first require inviting staff to self-report their ethnicity.
