# Four-day week: decision and pilot plan

**The proposal does not work as written.** Its two patterns put no showroom staff on Saturday, despite a minimum of six, including a key holder and two designers. The four showroom overtime volunteers could bring cover to four, including only one designer; volunteers from other teams are not trained to count. Using all four every Saturday would cost £617.40 per Saturday, or £7,408.80 gross over 12 Saturdays, and still leave a shortfall. Overtime is voluntary. “Saturdays as now” also conflicts with the fixed Monday–Thursday and Tuesday–Friday patterns.

Across 40 staff, contracted hours fall from 1,500 to 1,280 a week (14.67%) on unchanged pay. The survey's 87% is 13 of 15 respondents, not all staff: 37.5% responded and 32.5% of all staff said yes. The optional, unevenly distributed survey is not representative evidence of overall support; seek views from all teams before setting patterns.

**Options for Nina to consider:** retain current hours and rota; run the proposed hours and pay with a redesigned showroom rota; or change Saturday opening, minimum cover or staffing, subject to operational and staffing checks. Overtime alone cannot fix the written proposal. The following is a testable rota, not a recommendation to adopt the policy permanently.

**Pilot: 4 January–28 March 2027.** Keep the proposed 32 hours and pay; use A/B patterns for other teams, subject to checking their cover and staff arrangements. For showroom staff, the four days for each person are in `numbers.json`. Ella (S05) and Joy (S06) retain their agreed Saturday exclusions. The fixed rota covers the open days as follows:

Stagger unpaid lunches one at a time, preserving the required on-duty numbers and skills. Schedule weekday shifts 09:00–17:30 with a 30-minute unpaid lunch; Saturday shifts 08:00–16:30 with a 30-minute unpaid lunch, using the hour before opening for preparation. Each shift therefore has eight paid hours. This relies on agreeing the earlier Saturday start and the new patterns with staff. Plan trained relief for leave, sickness and breaks; the table is scheduled cover, not a guarantee of cover during absence. Check individual leave and bank-holiday treatment fairly before launch. Good Friday, 26 March, is closed; the pilot contains 12 Saturdays.

Before launch, agree baseline measures and targets with managers. Track weekly sales and completed appointments against comparable prior weeks, customer waiting times, errors or rework, staffing and skill cover, overtime and its cost, absence, and staff wellbeing and workload across all teams. Record missed cover and customer impact. Review weekly, adjust safely if cover fails, and consult staff at the midpoint. Meet on **Tuesday 30 March 2027**, after the pilot and Easter Monday closure, to compare results with the baseline and targets and decide whether to keep, change or stop the arrangement.
