# Board note: gender pay gap, 5 April 2026

## Publication requirement
We must publish gender pay gap figures. On the snapshot date we had **262 relevant employees**: direct employees, including part-time staff and people on leave. Agency workers are excluded. The legal threshold is 250 relevant employees, not 250 full-time equivalents, so the approximate FTE figure does not change the requirement.

## What the figures show
The hourly figures cover **248 full-pay relevant employees**. Fourteen employees are excluded because April pay was affected by maternity leave, sick leave, half-pay sickness or unpaid leave. Men’s mean hourly pay was **£20.07** and women’s was **£15.91**, giving a **20.8% mean gender pay gap**. Median hourly pay was **£14.68 for men and £14.08 for women**, a **4.1% median gap**. Positive gaps mean men were paid more. The larger mean gap suggests some highly paid roles have a strong effect; the median gives a better view of the middle of the pay distribution.

Women made up **79.0%** of the lower quartile, **66.1%** of the lower-middle, **72.6%** of the upper-middle and **58.1%** of the upper quartile. The chart shows the corresponding percentages for men and women.

For bonuses, **34.2% of relevant men and 28.4% of relevant women** received bonus pay in the year to 5 April 2026. Among recipients, men’s mean bonus was **£3,944.44** and women’s was **£889.42**, a **77.5% mean bonus gap**. The median bonus gap was also **77.5%**.

## Data notes
The ethnicity pay gap cannot be calculated reliably because the supplied HR export contains no ethnicity information. Sex was recorded in several formats, which I standardised to M and F. One person had a bonus payment and a matching reversal that netted to zero, so they are not counted as a bonus recipient. Hourly rates use April ordinary pay and contracted hours in force on the snapshot date; overtime, mileage and bonuses are excluded.
