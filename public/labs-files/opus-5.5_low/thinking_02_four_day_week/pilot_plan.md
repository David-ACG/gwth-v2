# Four-day week pilot: plan for the 15 October board

## Does the proposal work as written?
**Not for the showroom.** Patterns A (Mon–Thu) and B (Tue–Fri) leave **nobody on Saturday**, our busiest day. The minimum is 6, including 1 key holder and 2 designers.

Overtime doesn't fix it. Only **4 showroom staff** have volunteered (S02, S07, S09, S11), and only one of them is a designer. That would cost **£617.40 a Saturday, or £7,408.80 over 12 Saturdays**, and we'd still be below minimum.

**The "87% support" figure is weaker than it looks.** Only 15 of 40 staff responded (37.5%), so the Yes answers are **32.5% of all staff**. Only 3 of 12 showroom staff and 3 of 14 fitters replied.

Hours fall from 1,500 to 1,280 a week (**−14.67%**). **Good Friday (26 Mar 2027)** falls inside the pilot.

## Realistic options
1. **Company-wide pilot with its own showroom rota (below).** Other teams use Patterns A and B. No overtime is planned.
2. **Pilot for office, warehouse and fitting only.** Simpler, but it leaves out 12 people.
3. **The proposal plus overtime.** It misses minimum cover, costs about £7.4k and depends on goodwill.
4. **Delay to April.** Run a proper all-staff survey first.

## Measuring success
Compare against the same weeks in 2026:
- **Sales:** design appointments, conversion rate and order value (Saturdays separately)
- **Fitting:** jobs completed on time, and call-backs
- **Customer service:** response times and complaints
- **Cover:** days below minimum, and overtime cost
- **People:** sickness absence and turnover, plus a named all-staff survey at weeks 0, 6 and 12 (aim for at least 80% response)

Agree the pass thresholds before the pilot starts, for example "sales within 5% of baseline and no day below minimum".
