# Board note: gender pay gap at 5 April 2026

**Publication is required.** There were 262 relevant employees on the snapshot date, above the threshold of 250. The test is headcount, including part-time staff and staff on leave, not full-time equivalents. Agency workers are excluded. Of these employees, 248 were on full pay and are included in the hourly-pay analysis; 14 were excluded from that analysis because they were paid less due to leave. They remain in the headcount and bonus analysis.

Men's mean hourly pay was £19.37 and women's was £15.50. The mean gender pay gap was **20.0%** and the median gap was **5.0%**, both in men's favour. The mean is more affected by very high rates. These workforce-wide comparisons do not establish whether people receive equal pay for equal work.

Bonus gaps were **78.1%** at the mean and **73.5%** at the median, both in men's favour. A bonus was received by 34.2% of men (27 of 79) and 28.4% of women (52 of 183). These compare amounts actually paid in the 12 months to 5 April 2026, without adjusting for part-time hours.

Each pay quartile contains 62 full-pay employees, ranked from lowest to highest hourly pay:

| Quartile | Women | Men |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Lower | 77.4% | 22.6% |
| Lower middle | 71.0% | 29.0% |
| Upper middle | 74.2% | 25.8% |
| Upper | 53.2% | 46.8% |

We counted 15 repeated payroll lines with identical line IDs only once, applied a bonus reversal, standardised the sex labels and used the contracted hours in force on the snapshot date. The exports contain no ethnicity data, so an ethnicity pay gap cannot be calculated reliably; it is recorded as null.
