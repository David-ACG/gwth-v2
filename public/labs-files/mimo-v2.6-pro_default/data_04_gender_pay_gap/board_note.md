# Board note: gender and ethnicity pay gaps

As at 5 April 2026, 262 people were relevant employees. The publication threshold is 250 or more relevant employees, based on headcount rather than FTE. We therefore must publish gender pay gap information. The approximate 210 FTE total does not remove that obligation.

Among 248 full-pay relevant employees (77 men and 171 women), mean hourly pay was £20.08 for men and £16.00 for women, giving a mean gender pay gap of 20.3%. The median hourly rates produced a 3.8% gap. Positive gaps mean men were paid more. These figures compare hourly rates across all levels and do not show unequal pay for equal work.

Women made up 80.6%, 72.6%, 71.0% and 51.6% of the lower to upper quartiles; men made up 19.4%, 27.4%, 29.0% and 48.4%. Women were therefore more concentrated in lower-paid work.

Of 79 relevant men and 183 relevant women, 34.2% of men and 28.4% of women received a bonus. Mean and median bonus gaps were 78.1% and 73.5%, meaning bonus recipients who were men received substantially more.

Data checks: I normalised eight recorded sex formats to M or F, removed 15 exact duplicate payroll export rows with repeated transaction IDs so payments were counted once, and netted a £1,500 erroneous November bonus against its December reversal. Fourteen relevant employees were excluded from hourly figures because April payroll contained a qualifying leave-related line. An ethnicity pay gap cannot be calculated reliably because none of the supplied HR or payroll files records ethnicity, and the method note gives no alternative basis. Ethnicity and gender cannot therefore be compared from this data pack.
