# Thistlecombe Care: gender pay gap, 5 April 2026

We must publish our gender pay gap figures. The supplied payroll provider’s method sets the threshold at 250 relevant employees on the snapshot date, not 250 full-time equivalents. We have 262 relevant employees: 79 men and 183 women. Part-time employees and employees on leave count. The chair’s estimate of about 210 full-time equivalents does not remove this obligation.

Hourly pay calculations include 248 full-pay relevant employees (77 men and 171 women). Fourteen employees receiving reduced pay because of leave in the April monthly run are excluded from hourly figures, but remain in the headcount and bonus calculations.

Men’s mean hourly pay is £20.07 and women’s is £15.91. The mean gender pay gap is 20.8%; the median gap is 4.1%. These gaps use unrounded hourly rates. A positive gap means women’s pay is lower than men’s. The mean compares averages; the median compares the middle hourly rate for each sex. These workforce-wide figures do not establish whether people doing equivalent work receive equal pay.

The mean and median bonus gaps are both 77.5%, comparing recipients’ total bonuses. Bonuses were received by 34.2% of men (27 of 79) and 28.4% of women (52 of 183). Bonuses are not adjusted for part-time hours.

Each pay quartile contains 62 employees. Women/men percentages, from lowest to highest pay, are: lower 79.0%/21.0%; lower middle 66.1%/33.9%; upper middle 72.6%/27.4%; upper 58.1%/41.9%.

Data handling: excluded 14 agency workers, 18 leavers and five future starters; standardised the different sex labels; matched payroll IDs to employee references; and used contracted hours effective on the snapshot date, excluding later changes. Ordinary pay follows the method note, including salary-sacrifice deductions with their existing negative signs. Overtime, expenses and bonuses are excluded from hourly pay. Bonus totals use pay dates from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 inclusive, including the eligible off-cycle payments and netting the recorded reversal. Quartiles use unrounded rates, with employee reference breaking exact ties.

The ethnicity pay gap cannot be calculated reliably: none of the supplied files records ethnicity. It is therefore null, not zero. Suitable ethnicity records and an agreed comparison definition are needed before the board can compare the gaps.
