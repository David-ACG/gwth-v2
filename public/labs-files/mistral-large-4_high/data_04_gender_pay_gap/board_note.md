# Gender pay gap: board note (snapshot date 5 April 2026)

## Do we have to publish? Yes.

The ~210 figure quoted to the board is our full-time-equivalent headcount, but the legal test counts **relevant employees** on the snapshot date: a headcount that includes part-time staff and people on leave, and excludes agency workers (their agency reports them). On 5 April 2026 we had **262 relevant employees**, above the 250 threshold, so we must publish.

## Headline figures (hourly pay, full-pay relevant employees, n = 248)

Mean hourly pay: men £19.37, women £15.50. **Mean gender pay gap 20.0%; median gap 5.0%.** Positive means men are paid more.

## Bonus pay (12 months ending 5 April 2026)

34.2% of relevant men and 28.4% of relevant women received a bonus. **Mean bonus gap 78.1%; median bonus gap 73.5%**, and men's bonuses are larger on average.

## Pay quartiles

Women are 77.4% of the lower quartile, 71.0% of lower middle and 74.2% of upper middle, but only 53.2% of the upper (highest-paid) quartile.

## What this means

The typical woman is paid close to the typical man (median gap 5.0%), but the mean gap is 20.0% because men dominate the highest-paid quartile and receive much larger bonuses. The gap is driven by who reaches senior, high-paid roles, not by unequal pay for the same work.

## Corrections and limitations

- Sex was recorded in eight formats (F, female, Woman, etc.); we standardised these.
- 15 duplicate lines in the payroll export (identical line IDs and amounts) were removed.
- One November bonus paid in error was reversed in December; the two cancel, so that person is not a bonus recipient.
- Excluded: 14 agency workers (not our employees), 18 leavers before the snapshot, 5 joiners after it, and 14 staff on maternity, sick or unpaid leave in April 2026 (excluded from hourly figures only; they still count towards bonus proportions).
- Bonuses are counted by pay date between 6 April 2025 and 5 April 2026; long-service bonuses paid on 4 April 2025 fall just outside that window.
- **Ethnicity pay gap: we cannot report this.** The HR export contains no ethnicity data, so the figure is recorded as null. We recommend adding ethnicity to the HR system so the board can compare the two gaps next year.

No names, National Insurance numbers or dates of birth appear in the output files.
