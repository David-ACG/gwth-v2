# Gender pay gap, 5 April 2026: note for the board

**Must we publish? Yes.** The test is headcount, not full-time equivalents. Every employee counts as one, including part-timers and people on leave; only agency workers are excluded. We had **262 relevant employees** on 5 April 2026 (250 or more means publish). The "210 FTE" figure is the wrong measure, so the chair's position doesn't hold.

**Figures** (positive = men paid more; 248 full-pay employees, 77 men and 171 women)
- Mean hourly pay: men £19.37, women £15.50. **Mean gap 20.0%.**
- **Median gap 5.0%.**
- Bonus (all 262 employees): mean gap 78.1%, median gap 73.5%. 34.2% of men and 28.4% of women received a bonus.
- Women are 77.4% of the lower quartile, 71.0% of lower middle, 74.2% of upper middle and 53.2% of the upper quartile (chart attached).

**What this means:** women are about 69% of the full-pay workforce but hold a smaller share of the best-paid quarter. That drives the large mean gap: a few high earners, mostly men, pull the male average up, so the median gap is much smaller. Bonuses go more often to men and are far larger.

**Ethnicity pay gap: not calculated.** The files hold no ethnicity data, so it can't be worked out. Ethnicity would need to be collected, voluntarily, before this can be compared.

**Data corrections**
- 15 payroll lines were exact duplicates (same line ID); I counted each once.
- Sex was recorded in eight different spellings; standardised to M/F.
- Excluded 14 agency workers, 5 people who joined after the snapshot and 18 who left before it. Six people with leave dates after 5 April remain relevant.
- 14 people on maternity, sick or unpaid leave in April are relevant but not full-pay; they are in the headcount and bonus figures only.
- Contract hours use the row in force on 5 April, ignoring changes agreed for later.
- Bonuses counted by pay date, 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026: the 4 April 2025 long-service payments are out, the 2 April 2026 ones are in. A £1,500 reversal of a bonus paid in error was netted off.

**Caution:** the pay gap figures use a single month's pay, so one month's leave or overtime patterns can matter. Figures should be checked by Moorbeck before publication.
