# Thistlecombe Care: gender pay gap, 5 April 2026

**We must publish.** The supplied payroll provider's method sets the threshold at 250 relevant employees by headcount, including part-time staff and people on leave. We had 262 (79 men and 183 women). The reported approximately 210 full-time equivalents does not change this requirement. We excluded 14 agency workers, 18 earlier leavers and 5 later starters.

Hourly pay figures cover 248 full-pay employees: 77 men and 171 women. We excluded 14 people (12 women, 2 men) on reduced or unpaid leave; they remain in the headcount and bonus calculations. Three employees receiving full company sick pay remain included.

Mean hourly pay was **£19.37 for men and £15.50 for women**. The mean gender pay gap was **20.0%** and the median gap **5.0%**: women's mean and middle hourly rates were respectively that much lower than men's. These organisation-wide comparisons do not establish whether people doing the same work receive equal pay.

Women comprised **77.4%, 71.0%, 74.2%, 53.2%** of the lower, lower-middle, upper-middle and upper quartiles respectively. Each contains 62 people. Men's share is higher in the top quartile than in the other three; the chart shows both sexes.

For bonuses paid from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 inclusive, the mean gap was **78.1%** and the median gap **73.5%**, both favouring men. Bonuses went to 27 of 79 men (**34.2%**) and 52 of 183 women (**28.4%**). Bonus amounts were not adjusted for part-time hours.

We standardised sex labels, matched payroll references to HR, and removed 15 identical repeated payroll lines (12 April pay lines and 3 annual bonus lines). We used contracted hours effective at the snapshot, disregarding 26 later changes. Salary sacrifice reduced ordinary pay; overtime, expenses and bonuses were excluded from hourly pay. A £1,500 bonus reversal cancelled the original payment, leaving that employee with no qualifying bonus. Rates were calculated without intermediate rounding and ranked before reporting.
