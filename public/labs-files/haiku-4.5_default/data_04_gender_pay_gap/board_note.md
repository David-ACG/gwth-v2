# Gender Pay Gap Report: 5 April 2026

## Publication requirement

We **must publish** our gender pay gap figures. With 262 employees on the snapshot date (5 April 2026), we exceed the 250-employee threshold. The legal requirement applies to headcount, not FTE. Although our FTE is 209.52, our headcount of 262 triggers the reporting obligation under the Equality Act 2010 (Gender Pay Gap Information) Regulations 2017.

## Key figures

**Gender pay gap in hourly pay:**
- Mean gap: **21.0%** (men earn 21% more on average)
- Median gap: **3.7%** (smallest 50% of men earn 3.7% more than smallest 50% of women)

**Mean hourly pay:**
- Men: £20.78
- Women: £16.41
- Gap: £4.37 per hour

**Bonus gap (12-month period to 5 April 2026):**
- Mean gap: **77.5%** (significant disparity in bonus amounts)
- Median gap: **77.5%**
- Men receiving bonus: 34.2%
- Women receiving bonus: 28.4%

**Pay distribution by quartile:**
The chart shows the percentage of women and men in each pay quartile. Women are overrepresented in the lower-paid roles (79% of the lowest quartile) and underrepresented in the highest-paid roles (56.5%).

## What this means

The mean gap of 21% reflects women's concentration in lower-paid care assistant roles. The median gap of just 3.7% shows midpoint earners are nearly equal—the mean gap widens because more men hold higher-paid positions. The 77.5% bonus gap shows men receive significantly larger bonuses when awarded.

## Data notes

- We analysed 262 employees on the snapshot date (5 April 2026)
- 248 were in full-pay employment in April 2026 (14 were on leave-related pay)
- Ethnicity pay gap cannot be calculated as our HR system does not record ethnicity data
- All figures use April 2026 payroll data and contracted weekly hours effective on the snapshot date

## Next steps

We must publish these figures on our public website by 4 April 2027, including context and our action plan to close the pay gap.
