# Four-day week pilot: plan and options

## Does your proposal work as written?

Not quite. For office, warehouse and fitting staff, yes—they work Monday to Friday and the two patterns cover every weekday. For the showroom, there's a gap.

**The Saturday problem:** With fixed patterns (S01–S06 on Mon-Thu, S07–S12 on Tue-Fri), nobody is scheduled for Saturday. You currently rotate: six of ten Saturday workers each week, taking a weekday off. You can't run that with fixed patterns. Saturday would need voluntary overtime. Only four showroom staff (S02, S07, S09, S11) have volunteered. You need six. Shortfall: two.

## Your realistic options

**Option A (recommended): Fixed weekday patterns for showroom; Saturday as overtime.**

Use Mon-Thu/Tue-Fri as proposed. For Saturday, recruit two additional volunteers from the six current Saturday workers (S01, S03, S04, S08, S10, S12) to join the four explicit volunteers. Target: six people for Saturday overtime.

**Pros:** Closest to your original idea; fixed day off; tests overtime model; gives cost data.

**Cons:** Doesn't give Saturday workers a day off; costs ~£12,000 for pilot.

**Option B: Rotating week (Saturday built in).**

Saturday is part of the rota on rotation. In even weeks S07–S12 work Tue-Fri-Sat; in odd weeks S01–S06 work Mon-Thu-Sat. Everyone works four days; Saturday is always covered.

**Pros:** Everyone gets a day off; no overtime cost.

**Cons:** More complex; staff don't have a fixed day off.

**Option C: Reduce Saturday cover.**

Run fixed four-day weeks; reduce Saturday staff to four or five (minimum is six). Measure customer impact.

**Pros:** Simplest.

**Cons:** May disappoint customers.

## The pilot plan: my recommendation

**Working pattern:** S01–S06 work Mon-Thu. S07–S12 work Tue-Fri. All other teams on the same 4-day split.

**Daily cover:** Monday (S01–S06); Tue–Thu (all showroom); Friday (S07–S12); Saturday (six volunteers, overtime at 1.5×).

**Recruit Saturday team:** Before launch, ask the four explicit volunteers (S02, S07, S09, S11) and approach six current Saturday workers (S01, S03, S04, S08, S10, S12) to volunteer for the pilot. Target: six.

**Success metrics:** (1) Can you recruit six volunteers? (2) Staff satisfaction and absenteeism. (3) Do you hit Saturday appointment targets? (4) Is the overtime cost (£12,000 for 12 weeks) sustainable?

**Review:** Monday 30 March 2027. Decide whether to roll out, try Option B or C, or pause.

**Why Option A:** Your survey shows strong support. Fixed patterns match your fairness goal. This model tests sustainability and gives you cost data. If it fails, Options B and C remain available.
