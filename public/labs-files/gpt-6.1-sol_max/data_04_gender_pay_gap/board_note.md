Thistlecombe Care must publish its gender pay gap figures for 5 April 2026 under the supplied payroll provider's method. The 250 threshold counts people, including part-time employees and people on leave. There were 262 relevant employees (79 men; 183 women), so approximately 210 full-time equivalents does not remove the obligation. Agency workers, earlier leavers and later joiners are excluded.

Hourly figures cover 248 full-pay employees (77 men; 171 women). The 14 employees on reduced or unpaid leave remain in the headcount and bonus calculations. Mean hourly pay was £19.37 for men and £15.50 for women. The mean gap is 20.0%; the median gap is 5.0%. Women earned 20.0% less per hour on average than men; the median compares the middle hourly rates. Gaps use unrounded rates and do not establish unequal pay for the same job.

For bonuses paid from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, the mean gap is 78.1% and the median gap is 73.5%, both favouring men. Bonuses reached 34.2% of men (27/79) and 28.4% of women (52/183). Bonus gaps compare recipients' total payments, without adjusting for part-time hours.

Each pay quartile contains 62 employees. From lowest to highest, women represent 77.4%, 71.0%, 74.2% and 53.2%; men represent 22.6%, 29.0%, 25.8% and 46.8%. Women's representation is lower in the highest-paid quarter.

Data preparation: removed 15 exact duplicate payroll lines (12 April pay lines; 3 bonus lines), standardised sex labels and matched payroll IDs to employee references. Used contracted hours effective on the snapshot date, ignoring later changes. Ordinary pay deducts salary sacrifice and excludes overtime, expenses and bonuses; full sick pay is included. A £1,500 bonus reversal cancels the original payment, leaving that employee no qualifying bonus.

The ethnicity gap is null because no ethnicity data are supplied. A reliable comparison with the gender gap cannot be made.
