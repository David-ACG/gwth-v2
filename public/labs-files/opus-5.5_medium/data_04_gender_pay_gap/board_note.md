# Gender pay gap: snapshot date 5 April 2026

## Must we publish? Yes.

The threshold counts **people**, not full-time equivalents. That includes part-time staff and people on leave. On 5 April 2026 we had **262 relevant employees** (183 women, 79 men), which is above the threshold of 250. The figure of "about 210" is our full-time equivalent (209.5), which is not the test.

## The figures

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Mean pay gap | 20.0% |
| Median pay gap | 5.0% |
| Mean hourly pay, men / women | £19.37 / £15.50 |
| Mean / median bonus gap | 78.1% / 73.5% |
| Receiving a bonus, men / women | 34.2% / 28.4% |
| Women in lower / lower-middle / upper-middle / upper quartile | 77.4% / 71.0% / 74.2% / 53.2% |

A positive gap means men earn more. The typical (median) woman earns 5% less per hour than the typical man. The mean gap is much larger because a few of our highest-paid roles are held mostly by men. Women make up 71–77% of the three lower quartiles but only 53% of the top quartile. Men's bonuses averaged about £3,860, against about £850 for women.

The hourly figures cover 248 full-pay employees. Fourteen people on reduced pay in April because of maternity, sick or unpaid leave were left out of them. All 262 relevant employees are included in the bonus figures.

## Data corrections

- Fifteen payroll lines appeared twice with the same line ID (12 April pay lines and 3 March 2026 bonuses). Each was counted once.
- Sex was recorded in eight different formats. These were standardised.
- Some contracted hours changes take effect in May or June 2026. We used the hours in force on 5 April.
- Long-service awards paid on 4 April 2025 fall before the bonus period and were excluded. Those paid on 2 April 2026 were included. A £1,500 bonus paid in error and later reversed was cancelled out.
- Five staff who joined after 5 April were paid in April but are not counted.

## Ethnicity pay gap: not available

Our HR records do not include ethnicity, so this gap cannot be calculated. To report it, we would first need to collect ethnicity data voluntarily from staff and check that enough people respond for the figures to be reliable.
