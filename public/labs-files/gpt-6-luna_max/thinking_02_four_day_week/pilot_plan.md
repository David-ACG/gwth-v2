# Four-day week pilot decision plan

**As written, the proposal does not work for the showroom.** Its weekday split gives 6 staff on Monday and Friday, and 12 on Tuesday to Thursday, so weekday headcounts and skill cover are available. But all 12 patterns are weekdays: Saturday cover is 0 against a minimum of 6. The only showroom Saturday-overtime volunteers are S02, S07, S09 and S11. All four would provide only 4 staff, including just 1 designer; they cannot meet the Saturday minimum or skill mix. That overtime would cost **£617.40 per Saturday** (£7,408.80 over 12 Saturdays) and still leave the showroom short. Across all teams, hours fall from 1,500 to 1,280 per week, a **14.67% reduction at the same pay**; matching weekly output would require 17.19% more output per paid hour. The survey was 15 of 40 staff (37.5%); 13 Yes answers are 32.5% of all staff, not 87%.

**Options for the board:**

- Trial 32 hours over four 8-hour paid days at the same pay, with Saturday included as a contracted day. The schedule below repeats weekly; an alternative is to rotate Saturday assignments, with weekday pairings changed to preserve cover.
- Keep 37.5 hours over four days (9 hours 22 minutes 30 seconds paid each day). This preserves contracted hours but means long days and does not reduce total labour hours.
- Trial 32 hours only with trained relief/recruitment or changed opening arrangements for the uncovered hours. Overtime volunteers alone are not a workable Saturday solution.

**Pilot to cost and schedule:** 4 January–28 March 2027, 12 weeks. Keep pay unchanged and roster four 8-hour paid shifts per person. Repeat the showroom pattern in `numbers.json` weekly. Keep S05 and S06's childcare arrangements. In this pattern, showroom headcount is Mon 7, Tue 8, Wed 9, Thu 10, Fri 7 and Sat 7. Every weekday has at least two keyholders and two designers scheduled. Saturday has two keyholders and three designers; roster 7 so staggered breaks leave at least 6 on duty. Stagger weekday breaks too, keeping at least 5 on duty and a keyholder and designer available. Schedule an 8 paid-hour Saturday shift around the 09:00–16:30 opening (for example, 08:30–17:00 with a 30-minute unpaid break). The pattern assigns the same seven people to every Saturday, so confirm their agreement and monitor workload and fairness; a rotating version would need different weekly day pairings while retaining these cover checks. The showroom closes on Good Friday, 26 March; agree in writing how that closure and annual leave are treated under the temporary pattern before launch.

Use the 12 weeks before launch as the baseline. Track weekly sales and gross margin, design appointments completed and conversion, customer complaints and response times, fitting completion/rework, absence, overtime, and short staff pulses on workload, wellbeing and rota fairness. Set acceptable service, output and cost thresholds before starting; review the dashboard weekly and check showroom cover daily. Compare like-for-like weeks and account for the bank-holiday closure. The survey was optional and shared through a WhatsApp group, so treat it as a prompt for a representative staff consultation, not a mandate.

The pilot runs through Sunday 28 March. Hold the decision review on **Wednesday 7 April 2027**, after the Easter bank holidays, with the 12-week results, costs, cover record and staff feedback.
