# Thistlecombe Care: gender pay gap, 5 April 2026

**Publication:** Yes. There were 262 relevant employees on the snapshot date (agency workers excluded). The reporting threshold is 250 employees by headcount, including part-time staff; it is not a 250 full-time-equivalent threshold. The reported estimate of about 210 FTE therefore does not remove the duty to publish.

**Hourly pay:** 248 employees were full-pay relevant. Mean hourly pay was £19.37 for men and £15.50 for women. The mean gender pay gap was 20.0% and the median gap was 5.0%. Both are positive, so men’s hourly pay was higher on these measures; the mean gap is larger than the median gap because the average is more affected by higher-paid roles.

**Bonus pay:** The mean bonus gap was 78.1% and the median bonus gap was 73.5%, with positive values indicating higher average or median bonus pay for male recipients. 34.2% of relevant men and 28.4% of relevant women received a positive net bonus in the 12-month period. Reversals were netted against bonus payments.

**Quartiles:** Women’s and men’s shares within each pay quartile were: lower 77.4% women / 22.6% men, lower middle 71.0% women / 29.0% men, upper middle 74.2% women / 25.8% men, upper 53.2% women / 46.8% men. The chart shows the same distribution.

**Data notes:** The export used several labels for M and F; these were mapped to the corresponding categories. I removed 15 exact duplicate payroll rows before calculating pay. Ethnicity is not present in the supplied files, so an ethnicity pay gap cannot be calculated reliably; it is `null` in the JSON.
