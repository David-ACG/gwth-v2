# Gender pay gap — board note, 5 April 2026 snapshot

Thistlecombe Care must publish. The duty applies at 250 or more relevant employees on the snapshot date. This is a headcount, not full-time equivalents. We had 262 relevant employees on 5 April 2026: all employees, including part-time and those on leave. Fourteen agency workers, five people who joined after the snapshot, and eighteen who had already left were correctly excluded. The full-time equivalent figure of about 210 is therefore not the test.

Hourly pay uses 248 full-pay relevant employees. Fourteen people paid less than usual in April because of maternity pay, sick pay or unpaid leave are excluded from hourly figures but included for bonuses.

Mean hourly pay was £19.37 for men and £15.50 for women. Mean gap 20.0%, median gap 5.0%. Positive means men paid more on average. The large mean but small median suggests the gap is driven mainly by a small number of highly paid men at the top, while typical hourly rates are closer.

Quartiles (62 people each, lowest to highest): women were 77.4%, 71.0%, 74.2% and 53.2%. Women are the majority in every quartile but are least represented in the highest-paid quartile.

Bonuses in the year to the snapshot: 34.2% of men and 28.4% of women received one. Mean bonus gap 78.1%, median 73.5%, reflecting large annual bonuses for senior roles alongside small retention, referral and long-service awards.

Corrections: standardised sex entries recorded in eight formats; used contract hours in force on the snapshot, ignoring agreed May and June changes; counted each payroll line once after removing fifteen exact duplicate lines; netted a £1,500 bonus paid in error against its reversal; excluded overtime, mileage, and out-of-window bonuses per the method note.

Ethnicity pay gap cannot be given: the files contain no ethnicity data.
