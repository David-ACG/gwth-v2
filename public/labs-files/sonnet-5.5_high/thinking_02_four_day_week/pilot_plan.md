# Four-day week pilot: plan

The decision is yours; this sets out facts, options and one workable pilot.

## Does the proposal work as written?

**Not for the showroom.** Hours fall from 1,500 to 1,280 a week (-14.67%) on the same pay. Under Mon–Thu / Tue–Fri patterns, showroom cover is Mon 6, Tue–Thu 12, Fri 6, **Sat 0 against a minimum of 6**. Weekdays are fine.

Overtime doesn't close the gap. Only four showroom staff volunteered (Imran, Callum, Owen, Ben; Lewis is a fitter and can't count). That gives 4 on a Saturday: still 2 short, with one designer where we need two. It costs £617.40 a Saturday, **£7,408.80** over 12 Saturdays, and has happened twice in 2026.

The survey is weaker than "87%". It was self-selected through a WhatsApp group. 15 of 40 replied (37.5%), so the 13 yeses are 32.5% of all staff. Only 3 of 12 showroom staff replied. Also, 8-hour days are 30 minutes longer than now, which a fitter's comment worries about.

## Options

1. **Run as written.** Saturday unstaffed. Not viable.
2. **Four-day week for all, showroom rebuilt around Saturday** (below). No overtime needed.
3. **Start with office, fitting and warehouse (28 staff)**; showroom later. Lower risk, but leaves out the largest recruitment and Saturday question.
4. **Wait.** Survey all 40 staff directly, then start later.

If you want January, option 2 is the one that holds up on the numbers.

Every day meets minimum cover.

Points to settle:
- **Friday has one key holder** (S08). Train a second before January.
- **Saturday has one spare person**, so one absence is survivable.
- **A fixed Saturday team replaces the rotation.** S01, S10 and S12 would not work Saturdays. Consider rotating at week 6.
- **Saturday is 7 paid hours**, so Saturday staff would work 31, not 32. Decide how to make up the hour.
- **Ella and Joy:** check Mon–Thu fits their childcare arrangements.
- **Good Friday (26 March)** is the only bank holiday. Staff who work Fridays get it as a day off; Mon–Thu staff don't. Decide how to treat that.

## Measuring success

Record a baseline for October–December. Suggested measures, for you to set targets on:
- Showroom minimum met every open day, with any shortfall logged.
- Design appointments held, especially Saturdays, and orders won.
- Fitting jobs finished on schedule, and customer complaints.
- Sickness absence, overtime hours and cost.
- Staff pulse at weeks 6 and 12, sent to all 40 directly, aiming for 80% response.
- Applications for open roles.

Suggested pause trigger: showroom below minimum on two days.
