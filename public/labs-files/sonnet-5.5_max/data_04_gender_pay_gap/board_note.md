# Gender pay gap: board note (5 April 2026 snapshot)

## Must we publish? Yes

The threshold is 250 **employees**, counted by head, not full-time equivalents. On 5 April 2026 we had **262 relevant employees**: everyone employed that day, including part-timers and people on leave, but not agency workers. The chair's "about 210 FTE" is right (209.5 on a 37.5-hour week), but FTE is not the test.

## Our figures (positive gap = men paid more)

- **Mean hourly pay:** men £19.37, women £15.50: a **20.0% gap**.
- **Median hourly pay:** men £14.63, women £13.91: a **5.0% gap**.
- **Bonuses:** 34.2% of men and 28.4% of women received one. Mean bonus gap **78.1%**, median **73.5%**.
- **Women in each pay quartile** (lowest to highest paid): 77.4%, 71.0%, 74.2%, 53.2%, against 69.0% of all 248 in the pay figures.

## What this means

Pay is broadly similar for men and women in the same job title; the gap comes mainly from who holds which roles. Our five directors, the highest paid (£47 to £72 an hour), are all men. Without them the mean gap would be about 7.5%. They also received the five largest bonuses (£9,250 to £19,650); the largest to a woman was £4,300. This is not an equal pay finding.

## Corrections and gaps in the data

- Removed 15 duplicated payroll lines (12 in April 2026 pay, 3 bonuses); left in, they double-count pay.
- Used contracted hours in force on 5 April; later changes (May or June 2026) for 26 staff are ignored.
- Excluded 14 agency workers, 18 earlier leavers and 5 later joiners; 6 people leaving after 5 April are included.
- 14 people on maternity, sick or unpaid leave count as employees and in bonus figures, but not in hourly pay or quartiles. (248 is the pay-figures base, not the threshold count.)
- Bonuses are counted by pay date, 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. A £1,500 bonus reversed in December counts as none.
- Sex was recorded in 8 spellings; standardised to F and M.
- **Ethnicity pay gap: not calculated.** No file records ethnicity, so any figure would be invented. We would need to collect it first, voluntarily and with care.
