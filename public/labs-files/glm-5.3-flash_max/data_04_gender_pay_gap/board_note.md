# Thistlecombe Care — gender pay gap, 5 April 2026

## Must we publish?

Yes. There were 262 relevant employees on the snapshot date, so Thistlecombe Care is above the 250-employee duty to publish. The test is headcount, not full-time equivalent staff, so the roughly 210 FTE figure mentioned by the chair does not remove the requirement.

## The figures

Of the 262 relevant employees, 248 were full-pay relevant employees. The other 14 received statutory maternity pay, statutory sick pay, half sick pay, or had an unpaid leave deduction in April 2026. They remain relevant employees but are excluded from the ordinary hourly-pay figures.

Mean hourly pay was £20.07 for men and £15.91 for women, giving a mean gender pay gap of 20.8%. The median gap was 4.1%. A positive gap means men were paid more. The lower median suggests that high hourly rates among some men have a substantial effect on the mean.

In the 12 months to the snapshot date, 34.2% of relevant men and 28.4% of relevant women received a bonus. The mean bonus gap was 77.5% and the median bonus gap was 77.5%. This gap is not adjusted for part-time hours, as the regulations require.

Women made up 79.0% of the lower quartile, 66.1% of the lower-middle quartile, 72.6% of the upper-middle quartile, and 58.1% of the upper quartile. Women remain under-represented in the highest-paid quartile, though the concentration is less pronounced than the mean pay gap suggests.

## Data limitations and handling

We cannot calculate an ethnicity pay gap from these files because they contain no ethnicity field, so that figure is reported as unavailable. Sex labels were inconsistently recorded, so we standardised them to male or female. Agency workers, later joiners, and people who had already left were removed. We used the contracted hours in force on the snapshot date and ignored later changes. We found no missing contract hours or April payroll records for relevant employees.
