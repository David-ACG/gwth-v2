# Gender pay gap: board note

**Snapshot date: 5 April 2026**

## Must we publish? Yes

The legal test is the number of **relevant employees** on the snapshot date, not full-time equivalents. On 5 April 2026 we had **262 relevant employees** — everyone employed that day, excluding 14 agency workers (their agency counts them). That is above the 250 threshold, so we must publish our gender pay gap figures on our website and the government reporting service, with a director's signed statement, within 12 months of the snapshot. The chair's figure of about 210 is an FTE measure (our contracted hours total 209.5 FTE); it is not the test that applies.

## The figures

- Mean hourly pay: men £19.37, women £15.50 — a mean gap of **20.0%**.
- Median hourly pay: men £14.63, women £13.91 — a median gap of **5.0%**.
- Bonus: **34.2%** of men and **28.4%** of women received a bonus. Mean bonus gap **78.1%**; median bonus gap **73.5%**.
- Women in each quartile: lower **77.4%**, lower middle **71.0%**, upper middle **74.2%**, upper **53.2%** (see chart).

A positive gap means men are paid more. The large mean gap reflects more men in senior, higher-paid roles and higher male bonuses; the smaller median gap shows the typical man and woman are closer. Women are over-represented in the lower three quartiles; the top quartile is the most balanced.

## Corrections and things we could not work out

- 15 duplicated payroll lines (identical line IDs) were removed; leaving them in would have inflated pay or bonuses for 15 people.
- Hourly pay figures cover the **248** full-pay relevant employees. Fourteen were excluded because April 2026 shows maternity, sick or unpaid leave; they remain in the headcount and bonus figures.
- Bonuses use pay dates from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. This excludes annual bonuses paid in the April 2026 run (24 April 2026) and the 4 April 2025 off-cycle, and includes the 2 April 2026 off-cycle. One £1,500 bonus paid in error and reversed nets to zero, so that person is not a bonus recipient.
- **Ethnicity pay gap: not calculated.** No HR or payroll file records ethnicity, and the method note covers gender only. Reliable self-reported ethnicity data would be needed first; publishing a figure without it would be misleading.
