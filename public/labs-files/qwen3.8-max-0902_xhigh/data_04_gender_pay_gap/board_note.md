# Gender pay gap: board note, 5 April 2026

**Must we publish? Yes.** The chair's ~210 is a full-time-equivalent count (we are 209.5 FTE);
the legal test counts people. On the snapshot date we had **262 relevant employees** (79 men,
183 women) — all staff employed that day, including part-timers and people on leave, but not our
14 agency workers. Above 250, so we must publish, within 12 months, on GOV.UK and our website.

**Figures** (248 full-pay relevant employees)
- Mean hourly pay: men £19.37, women £15.50 — mean gap **20.0%**, median gap **5.0%**
- Bonuses in the year to 5 April 2026: 34.2% of men, 28.4% of women; mean bonus gap **78.1%**,
  median **73.5%**
- Women by quartile: lower 77.4%, lower middle 71.0%, upper middle 74.2%, upper 53.2%

Pay is fairly even through most of the organisation, hence the small median gap; the mean is
pulled up by a small, mostly male senior group (top quartile: £16.01 to £72.41 an hour). Women are
70% of staff and sit mainly in the three lower quartiles. The bonus gaps come from large annual
bonuses for directors and home managers in March 2026; most care staff get small retention,
referral or long-service payments. Fourteen people on maternity, sick or unpaid leave in April
2026 are excluded from the hourly figures, as the method requires, but count in the 262 and the
bonus figures.

**Corrections**
- The payroll export repeated 15 rows exactly (12 April 2026, 3 bonuses); removed, else 12
  people's pay would have doubled.
- Sex appeared in eight formats (F, Female, female, Woman, M, Male, "male ", Man); normalised,
  but I cannot verify the records.
- I used only contracted hours in force on 5 April 2026, ignoring 33 later changes.
- Bonuses count by pay date: 57 paid before 6 April 2025 fall outside the year; 10 paid on
  2 April 2026 fall inside. One £1,500 bonus was reversed (not a recipient); four recipients had
  left before the snapshot.
- Five people who started after the snapshot (13–20 April 2026) are in the April payroll; excluded.
