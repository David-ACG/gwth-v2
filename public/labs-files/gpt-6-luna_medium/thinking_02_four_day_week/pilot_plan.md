# Four-day week pilot: decision brief

**Does the proposal work as written?** No. The hours and pay change is clear, but a 32-hour week is a 14.67% reduction in contracted hours across the business (1,500 to 1,280 hours weekly) on unchanged pay. In the showroom, its fixed split leaves six people Mon, six Tue, and 12 Wed/Thu, but only six Friday and **none Saturday**. The Saturday minimum is six, including a key holder and two designers; the four showroom overtime volunteers could raise Saturday cover only to four, still two short. The proposal also treats survey support as 87% of staff: 13 of 15 respondents said yes, which is 32.5% of all 40 staff. The optional WhatsApp survey is a small, self-selected sample, not a reliable measure of all staff views.

**Realistic options for the board**

- Keep the five-day arrangement and improve recruitment/retention in other ways.
- Trial four days at 32 hours on the same pay, with a redesigned showroom rota and agreed cover arrangements. The rota below is one workable option: six people work Saturday as part of their four days.
- Consider a smaller or phased trial, or a different hours/pay arrangement, if the cost or service risk of a full 32-hour trial is too high. Cost and operational effects should be modelled before commitment.

**Suggested 12-week pilot (4 Jan–28 Mar 2027)**

Use the individual four-day showroom rota in `numbers.json`. It schedules six people Saturday (including key holders and designers) and at least seven each weekday, above the five-person minimum. Saturday workers take three weekdays; the other six take four weekdays. The showroom remains open six days, with one key holder and at least one designer weekdays, and one key holder plus two designers Saturday. Keep Sunday and bank holidays closed. The rota is a starting point: confirm leave, appointment demand, breaks, and staff agreement before launch. The weekday headroom helps cover absence; set a named, trained reserve process and avoid assuming voluntary overtime is guaranteed.

For the other 28 staff, agree team-level four-day patterns and handovers before launch, with customer service and fitting capacity covered each weekday. In particular, test fitting workload and customer response times; do not assume fewer hours can be absorbed without changes. Clarify pay, annual leave and bank-holiday treatment for the pilot in writing.

**Measure and review**

Record a four-week pre-pilot baseline, then review weekly: completed appointments and sales/conversion; wait time and missed appointments; showroom daily cover and absence; fitting jobs completed, delays and rework; customer complaints; overtime and total labour cost; and staff wellbeing, workload and fairness by team. Set numerical service and cost guardrails with managers before launch, report exceptions weekly, and ask all staff for confidential pulse feedback at weeks 4, 8 and 12. Meet on **5 April 2027**, after the 12 weeks, to compare results with baseline and guardrails and decide whether to stop, adjust, extend or keep the arrangement.
