# Gender pay gap: 5 April 2026

Thistlecombe Care must publish gender pay gap figures. The snapshot count is **262 relevant employees**, above the 250 employee threshold. This threshold counts employees, including part-time staff and people on leave; it is not based on full-time equivalent headcount. Agency workers are excluded. The quoted figure of about 210 FTE does not change the result.

There were **248 full-pay relevant employees** for the hourly pay and quartile calculations. Mean hourly pay was **£20.78 for men** and **£16.41 for women**, giving a **21.0% mean gap**. The median gap was **3.7%**. Both gaps use men's pay as the denominator; positive figures mean men's pay was higher. The larger mean than median gap indicates that high hourly rates among men have more effect on the average than on the midpoint.

The mean and median bonus gaps were both **77.5%**. **34.2% of relevant men** and **28.4% of relevant women** received a bonus in the 12-month period. These gaps compare bonus amounts among recipients; the percentages describe how many relevant employees received a bonus. Women made up **79.0%, 74.2%, 66.1% and 56.5%** of the lower, lower middle, upper middle and upper hourly pay quartiles, respectively (62 people in each quartile).

An ethnicity pay gap could not be calculated: none of the supplied files contains ethnicity data, so `ethnicity_pay_gap_pct` is `null`. Sex values recorded in different text formats were normalized to M/F for calculation. No relevant employee lacked snapshot contract hours or a usable sex value. Payroll IDs were matched to employee references using the mapping specified in the README. The hourly rates use the provider's April ordinary pay elements, leave exclusions, snapshot contracted hours and quartile tie break.
