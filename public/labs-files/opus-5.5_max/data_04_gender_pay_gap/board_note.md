# Gender pay gap: 5 April 2026 snapshot

## Must we publish?

Yes. The legal test is headcount, not full-time equivalent (FTE): everyone employed on 5 April 2026 counts as one, however few hours they work, including people on leave. We employed 262 people that day, above the 250 threshold. The chair's figure of about 210 is our FTE (209.5), which the law does not use.

We must publish on the government's reporting service and our website by 4 April 2027, with a statement signed by a director.

## Our figures

- Mean (average) gender pay gap: **20.0%**
- Median (middle) gender pay gap: **5.0%**
- Mean bonus gap: **78.1%**; median bonus gap: **73.5%**
- Received a bonus: **34.2%** of men, **28.4%** of women

All gaps favour men. Hourly figures cover the 248 employees on full pay in April 2026; the 14 on reduced pay because of leave are excluded, as the rules require, but still count towards headcount and bonuses.

Men's average hourly pay is £19.37, women's £15.50. The typical woman earns 5.0% less per hour than the typical man. The average gap is much wider because our five highest-paid employees are all men; without them it would be about 7.5%.

Women are 69.0% of full-pay employees, but 77.4% of the lowest-paid quarter, 71.0% and 74.2% of the middle quarters, and 53.2% of the highest-paid quarter (see chart).

Men's bonuses are much larger: the biggest annual bonuses go to senior roles mostly held by men, while women more often receive small flat-rate bonuses.

## What we corrected

- 15 payroll lines (£21,653) appeared twice, double-counting April pay for 12 people and three March bonuses. Removed.
- Five people in the April payroll started after 5 April. Excluded.
- 26 employees have hours changes agreed for May or June; we used hours in force on 5 April.
- An off-cycle bonus run labelled April 2025 was paid on 4 April 2025, before the bonus year, so is excluded.
- A £1,500 bonus paid in error and then reversed is cancelled out.
- Sex was recorded in eight formats; all standardised, none missing.

## What we could not work out

The ethnicity pay gap. Our HR records hold no ethnicity data, and guessing it from names would be unreliable and inappropriate. We would first need to ask staff to share it voluntarily.
