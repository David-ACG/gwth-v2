How models think about uncertainty

Large language models speak with confidence. Underneath, every word is a wager. Learning to read the wager — and to ask it back out loud — is the difference between using a model and being used by one.

A model isn't lying when it's wrong. It's predicting.

Most chat models are slightly over-confident: they say 90% when they should be saying 75%. You can correct for this at the prompt level by asking the model to estimate its confidence as a number…

Common questions

Things worth asking before you start.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. GWTH is built for non-technical adults first. If you write code already, you'll find the lessons useful too — but nothing assumes a technical background.

How long is a lesson?

Eight to twelve minutes of reading, plus an optional narration if you'd rather listen on the move. Most modules have twelve to sixteen lessons.

What does the score actually mean?

The GWTH Score is your current proficiency, measured against the live curriculum. It's not a percentage of right answers — it's a weighted view of which lessons you've completed, when, and how thoroughly. Read the full score guide →

What happens to my credential over time?

It doesn't expire. It gently decays if the curriculum has been updated and you haven't refreshed the changed lessons. Coming back and reading the updated material restores your score.

Can my employer verify my credential?

Yes. Every credential has a unique public URL and a QR code. Anyone can verify it without an account — they see your score, the modules you've covered, and when it was last refreshed.

Will it work on my phone?

Yes. The lesson viewer is built for phone, tablet, and laptop equally. Audio narration carries you on the commute; the reader takes over when you're sitting down.

Is there a free trial?

Yes — twelve lessons free, no card required. Long enough to know whether the format suits you.

What's not included?

One-on-one tutoring isn't part of the standard plan. Certification exams for outside accrediting bodies aren't either. The GWTH credential stands on its own.
