Section 01

Opening — the three old options

Hook Lesson page 02
~3 min ~480 wds

For most of the last twenty years, getting better at the practical bits of life usually meant one of three things: you learnt to do it yourself… you paid someone else to do it… or you quietly went without.

Tone

Open warm. Take your time on the three options — beat between each one.

Stage

Hold for a half-beat after "without" before continuing. Let the audience feel the weight of the third option.

That could mean drafting a difficult letter… making sense of a confusing bill… planning a project, getting your CV moving again, helping a family member understand an official form… or building a useful tool, workflow, or app you always assumed was beyond you.

Beat

Six examples — pace evenly. Don't rush. Each one is someone's actual life.

In the last two years, that has started to change. Ordinary people in the UK — not just engineers, founders, or people working in big technology companies — now have a new option. They can ask for help from AI, shape the answer, try again, and make something useful from a blank page.

Tone

Light lift on "two years" — the pivot of the lesson. From here, slightly more confident, slightly more curious.

Stage

The four verbs — "ask, shape, try again, make" — land each one cleanly. This is the lesson's quiet thesis.

This course is about that shift. And it has a much more ambitious end goal than simply helping you "try AI".

If you complete the course and do the projects, the aim is to put you in the TOP ONE PERCENT of people in the UK for practical, applied AI ability. Not theory. Not buzzwords. The real skill of using AI to research, write, analyse, build, automate, test, and create useful things that other people can see.

Tone

"Top one percent" should feel earnest, not boastful. The aim is grounded — practical ability.

Section 02

What this lesson is, and what it is not

Teach Lesson page 03
~2 min ~320 wds

Before we start, a quick word about what this lesson is, and what it is not.

Stage

Slight tonal shift here — more direct, slightly slower. You're setting expectations.

This is not a lesson about how AI works under the hood. It's not a history of machine learning. It's not a debate about whether AI is good or bad for society.

This is a lesson about how to use AI well — the way a craftsperson uses a good tool — to do things that mattered to you before AI existed, and now matter even more.

Tone

"Not… not… not…" — three short rejections. Quick, but not impatient. You're clearing the room.

Stage

"is a lesson about" — slow down here. This is the turn.

If you're new to AI, this lesson will give you the right map. If you've already used AI a lot, this lesson will give you better questions to ask.

Stage

Two short reassurances. Friendly, warm. The lesson is for both audiences.

Section 03

What GWTH stands for

Teach Lesson page 04
~2 min ~340 wds

GWTH stands for growth — the practical, slow-and-steady kind. Not the kind you see on a startup pitch deck. The kind you can hold in your hands at the end of a week and say: I made that.

Stage

"GWTH" — pronounced "growth", but cleanly spelled out by emphasis. The dropped W is the joke.

Tone

The hands moment — make this physical. "Hold in your hands at the end of a week" should feel solid, not abstract.

It also stands for what we believe about AI literacy. Ground it in real things you do. Work with the tool, not against it. Test it, instead of trusting it. Hold onto your own judgement, always.

Visual cue

SHOW FIG-02 — the four-word acronym poster — synced one word at a time as you read each letter.

Stage

Read each letter clearly. Slight emphasis at the start of each, like punctuation. Then the rest of the sentence at normal pace.

If you remember nothing else from this lesson — remember those four. They'll be quietly underneath every other thing we do.

Tone

Anchor moment. Slow down, look up if on camera. The line "they'll be quietly underneath" is the lesson's promise.
