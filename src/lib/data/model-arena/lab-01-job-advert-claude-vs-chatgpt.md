# Write a job advert: Claude vs ChatGPT

**Lab · HR** · Tested on 23 July 2026

Two AI tools, one real task, the same prompt to both. You read both answers,
score them against a simple rubric, and decide which one you would actually
use. The skill you are practising is judgement: knowing which tool did the
better job on a task you already understand.

## The task

You are an HR advisor at a small charity that runs a care home in rural
Shropshire. A Registered Nurse post has sat open for four months. The current
advert is dry and forgettable, and you think that is part of the problem. So you
hand the same tired advert to two AI tools and ask each one to rewrite it. Then
you judge the results.

## The matchup

- **Claude**: Claude Opus 4.8, run on 23 July 2026.
- **ChatGPT**: OpenAI GPT-5.6 (model id gpt-5.6-sol), run at high reasoning
  effort on 23 July 2026.

## The prompt (the same words to both)

Both tools were given this exact prompt, word for word.

```text
You are helping a busy HR advisor at a small charity that runs a care home in
rural Shropshire. We have been trying to fill a Registered Nurse vacancy for
four months with no luck. Here is our current job advert, which we think is
part of the problem:

Registered Nurse required. Applicants must hold a valid NMC pin and have a
minimum of two years post-registration experience. Duties include administering
medication, managing care plans, supervising healthcare assistants and liaising
with GPs and families. Shift work including nights and weekends is required.
Competitive salary. To apply, send your CV to recruitment@example.org.uk.

Please rewrite this advert so it is more likely to attract good candidates.
Keep it honest and accurate, keep it to about 250 words, use British English,
and make it warm and human without overselling the role. Include a short,
genuine reason a nurse might actually want to work here.
```

## The two answers

Both answers below are quoted exactly as each tool produced them. Nothing has
been tidied, shortened or corrected, so the spelling and punctuation you see are
each tool's own, not ours.

### Claude's answer

```text
Registered Nurse, small charity care home in rural Shropshire

We look after older people in a care home in rural Shropshire, and we are a
small charity rather than a large chain. We are looking for a Registered Nurse
to join us.

Here is the honest version of the job. You would lead the nursing care for a
small group of residents you come to know by name. That means keeping their
care plans up to date, giving medication safely, supporting and supervising our
healthcare assistants, and staying in touch with families and GPs. The role is
on a rota that includes nights and weekends, so some flexibility is needed, and
we would rather say that plainly now than surprise you later.

You will need a current NMC pin and at least two years of experience since you
registered.

The real reason nurses stay with us is time and continuity. In a busy hospital
you rarely find out how someone gets on after they leave your ward. Here you
care for the same residents week after week, so you actually see your work make
a difference, and you are given the time to do it properly. Being small also
means you are a person to us, not a number on a rota, and senior staff are on
the floor beside you rather than behind a door.

If that is the kind of nursing you came into the profession to do, we would love
to hear from you. Send your CV to recruitment@example.org.uk, or call us first
for a chat.
```

### ChatGPT's answer

```text
## Registered Nurse – Rural Shropshire Care Home

Are you a compassionate Registered Nurse looking for a role where you can build
meaningful relationships with the people you care for?

We are a small charity running a care home in rural Shropshire. We are looking
for an experienced Registered Nurse to join our team and help us provide safe,
thoughtful and person-centred care to our residents.

One genuine reason to work with us is the continuity: you will get to know our
residents, their families and your colleagues properly—and see the difference
your care makes over time.

Your responsibilities will include:

- Administering medication safely
- Reviewing and managing residents’ care plans
- Supervising and supporting healthcare assistants
- Liaising with GPs, families and other professionals
- Helping the wider team maintain high standards of care

You will need a valid NMC pin and at least two years’ post-registration
experience. Just as importantly, you will be calm, dependable and comfortable
communicating with residents, relatives and colleagues.

This is a shift-based role covering days, nights and weekends. We want to be
open about that from the outset, as flexibility is an essential part of
providing consistent care around the clock.

We offer a competitive salary and the opportunity to work in a close-knit
care-home team within a purpose-led charitable organisation.

If this sounds like the kind of nursing role you are looking for, please send
your CV to **recruitment@example.org.uk**. We would be pleased to hear from you.
```

## Score them yourself

Read both answers again and score each one against these six questions. There is
no right answer. You are scoring your own reading, the same way a hiring manager
would.

1. **Does it sound like a real person wrote it, or like a template?** Good looks
   like warm, natural sentences you could imagine a manager saying.
2. **Is it honest about the hard parts, like the nights and weekends?** Good
   looks like the shifts stated plainly and early, not buried or dressed up.
3. **Does it give a real, specific reason to want this job?** Good looks like a
   concrete reason tied to this workplace, not a vague perk like "competitive
   salary".
4. **Is the British English and punctuation clean for a UK advert?** Good looks
   like British spelling and no stray dashes or Americanisms you would have to
   fix before posting.
5. **Is it easy to skim in ten seconds?** Good looks like short paragraphs or a
   list, so a busy nurse can scan the key facts fast.
6. **Would you post it with only light edits?** Good looks like changing a
   detail or two, not rewriting it from scratch.

## The verdict

This one is close, and both rewrites are a big improvement on the original.

ChatGPT is the more polished advert at a glance. It opens with a clear heading,
lists the duties as bullet points, and would drop straight onto a job board. But
it leans on advert clichés the brief asked us to avoid, such as the "Are you a
compassionate Registered Nurse..." opener and phrases like "person-centred care"
and "purpose-led charitable organisation". It also used a dash in the heading
and in the body that a UK advert would tidy up.

Claude reads warmer and more human, and its genuine reason is stronger and more
specific: seeing the same residents week after week, being a person rather than
a number on a rota, senior staff on the floor beside you. It is honest about the
shifts in plain words. Its weakness is that it is all prose, so it is harder to
skim than ChatGPT's bulleted duties.

For this brief, which asked for warmth, honesty and a genuine reason without
overselling, Claude fits slightly better. The best real answer is to take
Claude's honesty and specific reason, then borrow ChatGPT's bulleted duties so a
busy nurse can skim it.

**Models change often, so this verdict is only true for these versions on this
date.** When newer models arrive we run the same prompt again and publish the
rematch. The archived version of this lab then shows how the tools compared back
in July 2026, which is a useful record of how fast they are moving.

## Try it yourself

1. Find a real advert for a role you know is hard to fill, or use the care home
   one above.
2. Open claude.ai and chatgpt.com. Both have a free option and neither needs
   payment for this.
3. Paste the exact same prompt into both, word for word, so the test is fair.
4. Read both rewrites and score each one against the six questions above.
5. Pick the one you would actually post, write one line on why, and note
   anything you would still change by hand.
