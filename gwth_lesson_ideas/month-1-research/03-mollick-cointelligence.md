# Ethan Mollick — Co-Intelligence (research spine)

Ethan Mollick (Wharton) is the most-quoted thinker on how humans should actually use AI day-to-day. His work is non-technical, applied, and opinionated — ideal for Month 1.

## The four rules (from *Co-Intelligence* and *One Useful Thing*)

1. **Always invite AI to the table.** Try it on every task you do.
2. **Be the human in the loop.** Interrogate the output; do your due diligence.
3. **Treat AI like a person — but tell it what kind of person.** A persona narrows its knowledge to the relevant slice.
4. **Assume this is the worst AI you'll ever use.** Capabilities still climbing.

## The intern metaphor

> *"An infinitely fast intern, eager to please but prone to bending the truth."*

It's creative, witty, persuasive — and will confidently fabricate when it doesn't know.

## The jagged frontier

Capabilities are uneven. AI is superhumanly good at some things (sonnets, persuasive copy, structured summaries) and oddly bad at others (counting words, spatial reasoning, very recent facts). You only learn the shape by *using* it. For beginners: don't assume; test.

The BCG consulting study Mollick co-authored remains the best empirical evidence: 700+ consultants, with-AI vs without-AI. Results:
- Tasks inside the frontier: **AI consultants out-performed by 40%.**
- Tasks outside the frontier: **AI consultants under-performed by 19%** (because they trusted the output).

Teaching implication: the risk *isn't* AI writing badly. It's you trusting it on tasks where it's bad.

## 2026 update — coworker framing

> *"Companies should stop thinking of [AI] as a better chatbot and start treating it as a system that can carry out hours of independent work."*
> — Mollick, March 2026

This is the agentic shift: from "prompt and reply" to "brief and delegate."

## Mollick's advice to beginners

- **Spend at least 10 hours using AI.** Below that threshold you can't form useful intuitions.
- **Try everything.** Every task, even small ones. Find your own jagged frontier.
- **Keep a persona library.** Personas you've found useful ("sceptical editor", "kind tutor", "pessimistic CFO") reusable across tasks.
- **Don't keep secrets from it.** The more context you give, the better the output.
- **Don't outsource judgement.** Always be the one who decides.

## Month 1 lesson touchpoints

- **L1 Welcome:** demo the jagged frontier live — two tasks, one stunning, one embarrassing.
- **L2 Mindset:** the four rules are the backbone of this lesson.
- **L4 Prompting:** persona priming ("tell it what kind of person it is") is step 1 of any prompt.
- **L21 Review:** revisit the four rules; students rate themselves.
