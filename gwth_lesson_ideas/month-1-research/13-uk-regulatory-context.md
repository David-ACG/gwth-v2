# UK AI Regulatory Context for Beginners (60-second version)

For L3 (tool choice + privacy), L5 (safety), L18 (family bot recording + children). Rule: **cite authorities, don't make students read them.**

## The five authorities every UK learner should know exist

### 1. ICO — Information Commissioner's Office
- Focus: data protection under UK GDPR.
- Published: **Guidance on AI and data protection** (2024, updated 2025), **AI & data-protection risk toolkit**, and an **AI strategic-approach** paper.
- Consumer-facing position (summarised by ICO in its *"AI-ll get that"* agentic-commerce blog, Jan 2026): **disclose when users are talking to AI.**
- Key consequence to teach: if an employee pastes client data into free ChatGPT, the employer is the data controller and carries the liability.

### 2. NCSC — National Cyber Security Centre
- Focus: security, including prompt-injection, data leakage, agent risks.
- Key blogs:
  - *ChatGPT and large language models: what's the risk?*
  - *Exercise caution when building off LLMs*
  - *Introducing guidelines for secure AI system development*
- Useful quote for L5 Rule 1: *"LLMs contain serious flaws, including the ability to get things wrong and 'hallucinate' incorrect facts."*

### 3. FCA — Financial Conduct Authority
- Focus: AI in financial services, consumer financial advice.
- Published feedback statements on AI use; monitors the consumer advice space.
- Relevance for Month 1: students using AI for "should I take this mortgage?" or "which ISA is best?" — AI output is *not* regulated financial advice.

### 4. AISI — AI Security Institute (DSIT)
- **Renamed from AI Safety Institute in 2025** under the Action Plan response.
- Tested 30+ frontier models in 2025.
- 2025 year-in-review published early 2026.
- Useful for positioning the UK as a credible global actor on frontier risks.

### 5. AI Opportunities Action Plan (Matt Clifford, Jan 2025)
- **38 of 50 recommendations delivered** at one-year mark (DSIT "One Year On", 29 Jan 2026).
- Tracking site: https://delivery.ai.gov.uk/
- £36 M into Cambridge DAWN facility → 6× supercomputer capacity.
- UK AI investment: £2.6 bn (2023) → £4.0 bn (2024) → £4.7 bn (2025).
- Roadmap pivots in 2026 to deployment, sandboxes, priority-sector acceleration.

## EU AI Act — UK-relevant dates

- **Article 50 disclosure ("you're talking to AI")** — August 2026 milestone for EU-facing tools.
- UK SMEs with any EU customer exposure need to comply, even though the UK is not in the EU.

## Bank of England signals (worth mentioning in Month 3, noted here for continuity)

- Feb 2026 AI roundtables; April 2026 FPC record: advanced AI *not yet* systemic risk in UK finance, but escalating fast.
- Feb 2026 Agents' summary: UK firms using AI/automation to reduce headcount growth.
- OBR Spring 2026 forecast: **£90 bn productivity swing** depending on AI delivery.

## Humphrey — the UK government's own assistant

- Launched 2025 as the UK civil service's internal AI assistant.
- Paired with "Extract", "Redbox", "Lex" and others across Whitehall.
- Useful teaching reference: students can see what their government built for civil servants and mirror the briefing style.

## Practical rules that fall out of the above (for L5)

1. **Disclose when content is AI-assisted** when relevant (client work, journalism, financial advice, political content, accessibility).
2. **Never paste PII, NHS numbers, client data, passwords into free-tier AI.**
3. **Don't record children under 13 without thinking about the Age Appropriate Design Code.**
4. **Financial advice from ChatGPT is not regulated advice.** Treat it as a research starter, not a decision.
5. **Prompt injection is real.** Don't blindly trust text pasted into agents from the web.
