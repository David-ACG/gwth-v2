# Task B: Mainstream alternatives to Amazon Quick

**Research cut-off: 10 October 2026. Audience: UK organisations with roughly 100 to 1,000 staff and a small IT team.**

## Scope, terminology and reading the prices

This is a comparison of business workspaces, rather than a ranking of underlying language models. The relevant capabilities are company knowledge search, research, analysis and artefact creation, plus agents that can act across applications. A product can be excellent at one of these and require additional products or implementation for the others.

**Evidence convention:** numbered citations identify the sources below. Vendor statements describe advertised or documented behaviour, not an independent certification that every deployment works that way. Suitability, strengths, weaknesses, setup ratings and recommended purchasing sequences are this report's judgement based on those documented capabilities. No installation was performed for this research. Measured implementation times for comparable vanilla UK rollouts were **not found**, so no invented number of days or weeks is supplied.

**Dates:** all sources were accessed on 10 October 2026. Publication and update dates are distinguished in the source list. Where a page supplies neither, its publication date is **not found**, and its freshness is unknown. Sources dated before 10 April 2026 are explicitly marked **older than six months, potentially stale**. A current help page is useful evidence of current documentation, but cannot establish the exact historical launch date of a feature unless it states one.

**Maturity:** GA means the vendor explicitly says generally available. GA with an allowlist still needs vendor enablement. Preview, beta and research preview are treated as restricted or unfinished offerings. “Available” on a current plan page means commercially listed, not proof of a separately dated GA announcement. Announced future capabilities are not counted as delivered. No rumoured feature is used to justify a purchase recommendation.

**Prices:** figures retain the vendor's published currency. No exchange-rate conversion is made. Annual prices are monthly equivalents with an annual commitment unless stated otherwise; monthly billing is given where verified. Base-suite licences, tax, implementation, security add-ons and agent consumption may be additional. A promotional price is not a permanent list price. “Not found” means no verifiable public figure or commitment was located, not that the feature or arrangement cannot be negotiated.

**Setup ratings:** low means little new infrastructure for a basic assistant in an existing suite; medium means connector consent, identity and content configuration across systems; high means substantial integration, information architecture, workflow design or procurement. These are comparative judgements. Permission remediation can dominate all three categories.

### Connections, permissions and governance

Native connections include Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive and Graph. Access follows user permissions; prompts, responses and Graph content are not used to train foundation models. Purview governs retained interactions, subject to configuration. Other systems require connectors or tools. [3]

### Vanilla rollout, fit, strengths and weaknesses

**Setup judgement: low to medium for native assistance; medium to high for safe cross-system actions.** Licence assignment can quickly expose useful email, meeting and document assistance in an established tenant. It cannot resolve ownerless SharePoint sites, obsolete policies, unrestricted links, missing transcripts or disconnected operational databases. Microsoft's own deployment guidance starts with content assessment, site lifecycle management and oversharing remediation. [87]

The natural fit is a Microsoft-heavy company whose staff already work in Outlook, Teams and Office and whose IT team can operate Entra, SharePoint and Microsoft administration. The practical strength is reduced application switching and a familiar identity/control plane. An organisation should expect stronger initial use cases around meeting recall, mail and document drafting than around unspecified “automate the business” ambitions.

The weaknesses are licence complexity, ecosystem dependence, variable content quality, and the need to manage a widening collection of agents and credit meters. Producing a spreadsheet analysis or presentation does not automatically deliver a governed, scheduled business dashboard connected to approved definitions. Compare accepted outputs on the company's own files and processes.

**Notable 2026 changes:** IQ branding and APIs, Cowork GA and consumption billing, current model-subprocessor choices, EU flex-routing controls, and updated Studio credit rules. All materially change an older “£23.10 buys everything” comparison. [4][5][6][7][9][10]

Protect includes basic oversight; Protect+ adds capabilities such as continuous classification and automated handling of overshared content. Hiding material in Glean does not repair the original application's ACL. Some agent-alignment/scanning capabilities are beta. It would be misleading to assume all marketed governance functions are included in the entry contract. [52][55]

### Vanilla rollout, fit and assessment

**Setup judgement: medium to high.** There is a substantial difference between connecting one well-maintained source and indexing the organisation's collaboration, service, CRM and document systems. The small IT team needs source owners to help resolve permission and content problems; a provider or implementation partner can assist, but cannot decide which policy is authoritative.

Glean best suits an organisation with many SaaS systems, costly knowledge fragmentation and enough budget to justify a dedicated enterprise layer. The strengths are a consistent search/assistant experience across ecosystems and a more explicit governance/knowledge proposition than many general chat products. Weaknesses are quote-based economics, integration work, possible advanced-security add-ons and further consumption complexity. It is harder to justify if most useful work already sits in one office suite and the company has no identified cross-source problem.

**Notable 2026 changes:** increasingly autonomous agents, agent-specific governance, and current Flex/Core pricing documentation. The product should be assessed as an agent platform with knowledge infrastructure, not simply “search with an LLM attached”. [53][54][55]

These are recommended evaluation sequences, not claims that a vendor wins every task.

### The Microsoft-shop case, honestly

For many UK medium-sized businesses, Microsoft is already part of their licence and partner discussion. A recommendation that ignores Copilot's position inside their existing identity, Office and collaboration environment is incomplete. It often has a lower organisational switching cost than introducing a new universal workspace. That is a procurement and adoption advantage, not proof of superior output quality.

Start by distinguishing **the included chat entitlement, paid seats, Studio/agent consumption and premium governance services**. A reseller's combined quote should identify each component. Test whether a paid seat adds value over included chat for the actual role. A meeting-heavy project manager, a document-heavy consultant and a warehouse supervisor need not receive the same entitlement.

Make the first comparison concrete: produce an accepted customer briefing, retrieve the current policy with evidence, summarise an actual meeting, and analyse an approved workbook. Check whether the tools retrieve the right material under ordinary staff permissions. If the main failure is missing or badly shared content, changing model brands will not cure it.

Paying for a second assistant can still be rational. For example, a department may produce significantly better accepted work in Claude or ChatGPT, while the organisation uses Copilot for meetings and Office assistance. Require the department to show incremental value and define which platform may access which data. Multiple workspaces should be an explicit operating decision, not an accumulation of overlapping subscriptions.

### What every vanilla pilot should resolve

Before company-wide purchasing, select a small set of recurring tasks and identify their information sources, responsible owners, outputs and failure costs. Establish a baseline for completion time and quality. Then evaluate the candidate under normal staff identities, rather than the founder's or IT administrator's unusually broad access.

For knowledge tasks, include questions with an authoritative answer, contradictory/obsolete documents, missing information and content the test user must not see. For actions, start with drafts and staged changes, then test approvals, duplicate events, revoked access, failed integrations and recovery. This is the difference between a useful demonstration and an operable process.

A founder's carefully connected account can conceal the hardest rollout issues: other staff have different entitlements, incomplete source access and less knowledge of how to check an answer. “Works extremely well for me” is evidence for a promising use case, not yet evidence for a vanilla organisation-wide experience.

## 9. Real-world adoption, value and licence wastage

### 9.1 What the strongest relevant evidence actually measures

| Source and publication date | Setting and method | Findings relevant to this lesson | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK cross-government report, **2 June 2025** | Around 20,000 participants across 12 organisations; **30 September to 31 December 2024** trial | Widely quoted **26 minutes/day** estimated saving; benefits concentrated in particular activities | Self-reported saving; older than six months; tests an older Copilot, not October 2026 agents. [78] |
| DWP evaluation, **29 January 2026** | **3,549 licences**; 1,713 survey responses; trial October 2024 to March 2025 | Adjusted estimate **19 minutes/day**; among respondents 65% daily and 30% weekly use | Model-adjusted self-report, not a directly timed causal estimate; publication and tested product now old. [79] |
| HMRC evaluation, **9 July 2026** | 3,000 randomly allocated licences plus 500 volunteers; **September to December 2024**; survey and task evidence | **83% used at least once**; estimated **60 minutes/week**, around 2 to 3% of working time | Random allocation does not make the self-reported savings objective; 1,364 survey responses, 45% response; fresh publication, older product trial. [80] |
| DBT evaluation, **28 August 2025** | 1,000 licences; October to December 2024; roughly 70% volunteers; diary, interviews and observed tasks | Good satisfaction for some written/meeting tasks; weak results for some presentation/scheduling uses | No overall counterfactual evaluation; diary response 32%; task-specific, not an all-purpose productivity multiplier. [81] |
| Dillon et al., **15 April 2025**, 56-company experiment | Randomised access for more than 6,000 workers over six months, with usage telemetry | Average **38% weekly use**, varying roughly 9 to 75% across firms; changes in email and document activity | Microsoft-affiliated research, not vendor-independent; older than six months; telemetry does not establish total business productivity or output quality. [82] |
| ChatGPT Enterprise adoption paper, **12 August 2026** | More than 1,500 organisations and 17 million messages; adoption patterns over six-month horizons | Evidence of sustained and uneven organisational usage, not just consumer experimentation | Vendor-affiliated authors; observational patterns are not causal evidence of financial return; not a vanilla UK mid-market trial. [83] |
| Autor et al. patent-drafting trial, **September 2026**, posted 9 September and revised 21 September | Preregistered three-month randomised trial: **133 lawyers at 11 US firms**, blinded expert scoring | AI-assisted quality improved at short and longer assessment points; later unaided benefits concentrated among experienced lawyers | Google collaboration and multiple Google authors; a custom assistant, not a standard Gemini Workspace rollout; working paper. [84][85] |

These studies should not be averaged into “AI saves X minutes”. They involve different work, uptake, measurement and products. In particular, a report's publication date does not identify the product version or the period when participants used it.

The 56-company experiment found a smaller intent-to-treat email effect than its effect among actual users: about **12 fewer minutes on email per week, 7%**, across those assigned access, versus approximately **30 minutes, 18%**, among users. Document completion changed too, but neither measure is a universal productivity figure. Quoting a users-only effect as the return on every purchased licence overstates the business case. [82]

The patent study is useful because it evaluates **quality and subsequent unaided judgement**, not only perceived time savings. The authors found improvements of **0.34 standard deviations at ten days and 0.38 at ninety days** in assisted drafting. Later unaided gains were concentrated among senior practitioners; juniors did not show an average unaided improvement. A leader should therefore evaluate review quality and staff learning, not merely faster drafting. [85]

### 9.2 Independence and evidence gaps

The government evaluations are customer-side evidence independent of Microsoft's sales organisation, although conducted by the deploying departments and subject to the limitations above. The Microsoft field experiment, OpenAI enterprise adoption paper and Google patent collaboration have vendor involvement. Their affiliation does not make the findings useless, but it prevents describing them as wholly independent validation.

Google has also promoted Workspace/Gemini survey comparisons using Hypothesis Group. Such material can identify perceptions and useful hypotheses, but a vendor-promoted survey is not equivalent to a randomised productivity comparison between current Workspace and current Copilot in similar organisations. [86]

A robust, independent, controlled comparison of **vanilla October 2026 Copilot, Gemini Enterprise/Workspace and ChatGPT Enterprise deployments in UK companies with 100 to 1,000 staff was not found**. A transferable independent percentage for licence wastage across these exact products was also **not found**. The evidence is strongest for specific tasks, early adoption and reported assistance, and weaker for realised cash return from a complete autonomous workspace.

Vendor case studies often describe customers with executive sponsorship, champions, selected roles, prepared information and partner assistance. They are useful examples of a supported deployment, but not automatically evidence for the outcome of simply buying licences. This research does not turn a customer success story into an installation guarantee.

### 9.3 Adoption curves and unused seats

A universal “normal adoption curve” with a verified week-by-week percentage was **not found**. The available evidence supports a more practical view: activation, repeated use and workflow dependence are separate stages, and organisations vary widely. HMRC's at-least-once use figure leaves **17% who did not use it at all during that trial**, calculated as 100% minus 83%. It does not identify why, nor prove every occasional user was a wasted licence. The 56-firm weekly-use spread shows why a single benchmark is inadequate. [80][82]

For a vanilla rollout, this report recommends measuring four separate things: licensed users, monthly active users, repeated use on an identified task, and accepted work completed. Logins and prompt counts alone can reflect curiosity or repeated failed attempts. Some low-frequency use is valuable, but it needs a task-specific justification.

Review licences regularly, reassign seats within contractual rules, and preserve an included/lightweight route for occasional users. Base purchase decisions on recurring workload and observed benefit, not job seniority or enthusiasm. Annual commitments can leave the business paying for unused seats even after user access is removed, so renewal and reduction terms matter.

### 9.4 Turning saved time into value

Use a task-level business case:

**Net benefit = value of accepted additional capacity or improved outcomes minus licences, agent usage, implementation, training, review, correction and ongoing administration.**

Time saved is not automatically cash saved. A faster draft can create capacity without reducing payroll; a bad draft can consume someone else's review time. HMRC's report discusses an extrapolated potential financial value, which should not be presented as already realised cash savings. [80]

Record the quality threshold, reviewer effort and intended use of released capacity before scaling. For dashboards, check authoritative metrics, source freshness and refresh operation. For research, check citations and omissions. For automation, check successful completion and safe recovery. These measures make the case stronger than multiplying a vendor's claimed daily saving by every member of staff.

## 10. Known risks and how they affect a vanilla rollout

### 10.1 Oversharing through existing or incorrectly mapped permissions

An assistant can respect permissions perfectly and still expose a sensitive document to a user who was mistakenly given access years ago. Faster discovery changes the practical exposure of forgotten shared material. The same problem applies to SharePoint, Drive, Confluence, Slack and uploaded/shared workspace files.

Microsoft explicitly identifies broad links, excessive audiences, broken inheritance, ownerless sites and weak protection as Copilot preparation issues. Its connector documentation also demonstrates a separate integration-created problem: “visible to everyone” indexing can broaden visibility relative to the intended source ACL. These are documented operational risks, not speculation that a model necessarily bypasses access controls. [87][88]

**Named, independently verified ordinary-customer oversharing incidents and a defensible incident rate for these products were not found in the sources reviewed.** Security demonstrations below establish exploitable failure modes; they do not establish how many businesses have suffered a breach. Avoid teaching an anonymous internet anecdote as a confirmed organisation-wide incident.

Recommended controls are to identify authoritative sources and owners, remediate broad access, test retrieval with ordinary and restricted identities, verify connector ACL mapping, and reassess shared outputs. Restricting discovery can help during remediation, but should not be confused with repairing the underlying access policy.

### 10.2 Prompt injection through connected knowledge and tools

A document, email or web page can contain malicious instructions intended for the agent. The user may be legitimately allowed to read that material; ACL enforcement therefore does not settle whether its instructions should be followed. Combining retrieval with browsing, messages or other tools creates paths to disclose information or execute unwanted actions.

| Documented case | What was demonstrated | Current-status qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **EchoLeak, CVE-2025-32711** | A crafted email could cause zero-click data exfiltration through Microsoft 365 Copilot, crossing instruction/data boundaries | Historical 2025 vulnerability, discussed in a September 2025 research case study; older than six months. It is not evidence that the same exploit remains available in October 2026. [89] |
| **ShadowLeak** | Radware demonstrated indirect injection through connected Gmail with ChatGPT research/web capabilities, with leakage originating from the cloud service | **18 September 2025** advisory says OpenAI confirmed and fixed it. Historical, not a claim of an unpatched current vulnerability or a named customer's breach. [90] |
| **SearchLeak, CVE-2026-42824** | Varonis demonstrated a one-click Microsoft 365 Copilot Enterprise Search chain combining prompt injection and web-security weaknesses to extract connected information | Article last updated **15 June 2026** says Microsoft remediated it. Research demonstration, not proof of real-world exploitation prevalence. [91] |

The lesson is to evaluate the whole retrieval/tool/action path, not to conclude that all connectors must be removed or that an output filter guarantees safety. Vendor mitigations, including Workspace Studio's restricted scopes and confirmations, reduce risk but still need correct configuration and representative testing. [92]

Begin with read-only knowledge access where practical. Separate reading from sending, publishing, modifying and deleting. Require meaningful approval for external messages and consequential writes, constrain tools/credentials, and test malicious source content in a controlled environment. Review the cloud-side audit trail, since endpoint/network logs may not show every agent action.

### 10.3 Agent sprawl and unclear responsibility

Low-friction building creates many agents, plugins, scheduled tasks and service accounts. Without ownership, an automation can continue after its creator changes job, a policy changes or its original use case disappears. A shared credential can make the problem worse by hiding which person's authority is being exercised. [31][43][55]

Maintain an agent inventory with purpose, owner, approved sources, credential identity, permitted actions, schedule, spend limit, review date and a method to stop it. Distinguish a personal assistant from a shared agent and an autonomous service process. Assign accountability for outputs and failures before giving an agent external or financial authority.

Check what disabling actually does. Revoking a workspace app approval may not revoke source OAuth authorisation; a local computer becoming offline is not the same as revoking an access grant; a new beta may have different audit coverage from an established service. [31][39]

### 10.4 Cost creep

The commercial trend is a combination of seats and multiple consumption meters. Longer reasoning, repeated failed runs, premium models, search/grounding, background schedules, voice, compute and indexed storage can all affect the bill. A free chat seat or included AI feature does not establish zero marginal cost for an autonomous process. [6][7][16][40][53][54][60][63][67]

Cost agents by **accepted completed business task**, including review and retries. Obtain a sample bill for a representative workflow, set owner/team budgets, monitor consumption separately from seat adoption, and decide what pauses automatically at a limit. Check whether a budget merely alerts or actually stops execution. Confirm prepaid-pack, overage, rollover and renewal rules rather than treating the word “credits” as comparable across vendors.

### 10.5 Data governance and residency misunderstandings

Assess four separate locations: the original connected system, the assistant's stored content/index, the inference/execution service, and external tools or destinations. Add retention, backups, operational metadata, subprocessors and audit/export support. “Not used for training” is a purpose limitation, not a statement that nothing is retained or that processing remains in the UK.

Important current examples are Microsoft's flex routing and Anthropic exclusions; Google's feature/model-limited UK allowlist; ChatGPT Business's limited US safety copy and Dots' beta exclusions; Claude's direct-SaaS US storage; and Notion's distinction between European storage and processing. They prevent a single yes/no residency badge from being an adequate procurement answer. [4][5][15][33][39][49][69]

Ask for a written architecture and commitment covering the precise edition, model, region, connector and agent experience being bought. A residency requirement can change the product configuration, price and feature set. No blanket conclusion about UK GDPR compliance follows solely from a storage-region label.

## 11. Implications for the GWTH lesson

Teach learners to choose an initial workspace from their existing work environment, then evaluate the exception cases. Avoid a universal winner: the strongest choice for Teams/Office assistance can differ from the strongest choice for fragmented knowledge, intensive research or CRM process execution.

Use a vanilla-rollout exercise in which learners compare the same task under a normal employee account, a source owner's account and an administrator's account. Make them identify the authoritative document, the relevant permission boundary, the review step and the full price of repeating the task. This directly addresses why a founder's excellent personal setup may not transfer unchanged to the workforce.

Make the distinction between **assistant, connected knowledge workspace and operational agent** explicit. Each expansion brings additional value and additional requirements. The adoption leader should leave the lesson able to explain what the first licence delivers, what setup remains, which features are still beta, and what evidence would justify the next purchase.
