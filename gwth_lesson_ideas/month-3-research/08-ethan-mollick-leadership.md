# Ethan Mollick — AI Leadership, Lab & Crowd

**Primary sources:** Prof Ethan Mollick (Wharton), *Co-Intelligence* (book), *One Useful Thing* (Substack), various 2026 talks.

## Core arguments relevant to Month 3

### 1. You cannot outsource AI transformation

> *"Nobody has special information about how to best use AI at your company. Consultants especially don't know your industry, organization, or context."*

The old playbook — hire McKinsey / Accenture / EY to run the transformation — is dead for AI. The reason: AI adoption is *organisational learning*, and learning cannot be outsourced.

Consultants can still help with process mapping, change management, and delivery. But *the question of how to use AI in your company must be answered from inside the company.*

### 2. Leadership / Lab / Crowd

Mollick's framework for how organisations learn to use AI fast:

- **Leadership** — sets the ambition, removes the blockers, models fluency.
- **Lab** — a small central team that builds, tests, and codifies what works.
- **Crowd** — every individual experimenting in their own work.

Organisations that do all three, with feedback loops between them, learn faster than competitors. Organisations that leave it all to "the crowd" (individual experimentation, no sharing) or all to "the lab" (central team, no grassroots) stall.

### 3. Five rules of great AI leadership

Surfaced in multiple talks and Insight Partners interviews:

1. **Leaders must be hands-on users.** You cannot lead what you have not tried.
2. **Invest in the experimental lab.** A small team, a real budget, a clear charter.
3. **Give time and permission to the crowd.** Individuals need hours in the week to experiment.
4. **Share across the crowd.** Private breakthroughs are wasted. Build a forum (channel, wiki, playbook).
5. **Redesign, don't retrofit.** Workflows must be redesigned for AI, not decorated with AI.

### 4. The jagged frontier

Mollick's influential framing: AI capability is *not* a smooth curve. It is jagged — exceptional at some tasks, useless at neighbouring ones. The implication for leaders: you cannot reason about "AI can do X" from "AI can do Y". You have to test.

## Quotes to use

> *"Organizations where leadership abdicates responsibility and delegates AI adoption to consultants or councils consistently see AI adoption stall."*
>
> *"Major firms like McKinsey and Ernst & Young don't know how to use AI for your systems. Organizations have to build the systems themselves."*
>
> *"Treating AI adoption as an organizational learning challenge requires building feedback loops between Leadership, Lab, and Crowd."*

## How to use this in GWTH

- **Core argument against "we'll hire someone to do this for us".** GWTH participants are *exactly* the leadership layer Mollick is talking to. The course itself is a tool for exactly the in-house capability build Mollick prescribes.
- **Leadership/Lab/Crowd as a diagnostic.** Week 1 of Month 3 has participants map where their org is on each of the three.
- **Permission-giving for experimentation.** Many SME leaders feel they shouldn't spend time "playing". Mollick's argument — they must.
- **Ties naturally to "train your team with GWTH".** If you can't outsource it and the crowd needs permission to experiment, the CEO's job is to give the crowd the training and the hours.
