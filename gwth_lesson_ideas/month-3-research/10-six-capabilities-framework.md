# The Six Capabilities — Synthesised Framework

**Primary source:** *Rewired* 2nd ed + McKinsey companion articles.

## Why synthesise

The six capabilities is the spine of *Rewired* and the most durable framing in McKinsey's Tech & AI body of work. Every lesson in Month 3 lives against one or more of these six. This page is a one-stop reference.

## The six capabilities — at a glance

| # | Capability | The question it answers |
|---|-----------|------------------------|
| 1 | **Roadmap** — transformation that targets real value | *What are we actually trying to change, in what order, for what value?* |
| 2 | **Talent** — a bench of highly skilled experts | *Do we have the people to do this, inside our walls?* |
| 3 | **Operating model** — that moves at pace | *Can our structure actually decide and ship as fast as we need to?* |
| 4 | **Technology** — distributed, flexible, scalable | *Does our stack let the people above do their jobs?* |
| 5 | **Data** — embedded throughout | *Is our data usable, discoverable, protected, and generating advantage?* |
| 6 | **Adoption & scaling** — realising meaningful gains | *Are the people who should use this actually using it? At scale?* |

## The rule of non-optionality

> *"A good operating model can't work without the right talent."* — Rewired 2nd ed.

You cannot skip a capability. They are interdependent. An excellent AI platform (capability 4) with poor adoption (capability 6) delivers zero value. Excellent data (capability 5) with no roadmap (capability 1) produces expensive warehouses.

The McKinsey finding: the leading 20% of companies have built a *baseline of competence* across all six. The laggards are strong in one or two and blind to the rest.

## The 1:1 investment rule

> *"For every $1 spent on developing digital and AI solutions, plan to spend at least another $1 to ensure full user adoption and scaling across the enterprise."* — McKinsey.

Most programmes underfund capability 6 (adoption). This is the single most common failure mode.
