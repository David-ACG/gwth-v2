# Institutional AI vs Individual AI — George Sivulka (a16z)

**Primary source:** George Sivulka, *a16z News*, Mar 12 2026.
**URL:** https://www.a16z.news/p/institutional-ai-vs-individual-ai

## Core thesis

> *"Productive individuals do not make productive firms."*

AI has made every individual employee roughly 10× more productive. And yet no company has become 10× more valuable. The gap is not a mystery — it's institutional. Individual productivity without coordination creates chaos, not output. To translate individual AI gains into institutional value, firms must redesign themselves around AI. The analogy Sivulka uses is electricity: textile mills didn't get productivity gains for 30 years until they restructured the *building* — decentralised motors, assembly lines — around the new power source.

## The seven pillars of institutional AI

Where individual AI ends, institutional AI begins.

1. **Coordination.** Individual AI creates chaos; institutional AI orchestrates agents with defined roles and responsibilities. *"Thousands of agents or humans rowing in opposite directions creates a standstill at best."*
2. **Signal.** Individual AI generates noise (50 deals instead of 10); institutional AI filters it. Deterministic, auditable processes select the right thing.
3. **Bias / Objectivity.** Consumer AI is "over-aligned" — it agrees with the user. Institutional AI must challenge assumptions, surface risks, enforce standards — the way boards and auditors do.
4. **Edge.** Domain-specific, purpose-built AI will always beat generalist models on the specific task. Competitive advantage lives in the specific.
5. **Outcomes.** Individual AI saves time; institutional AI scales revenue. Leadership cares about growth, not minutes.
6. **Enablement.** Individual AI hands out tools. Institutional AI includes change management, process engineering, and adoption as first-class work.
7. **Unprompted action.** Individual AI waits for a prompt. Humans rarely know the right question. Institutional AI surfaces risks and opportunities before being asked.

## Supporting framings

- **The electricity analogy.** 30-year lag between electrifying mills and seeing gains — *because the institution didn't yet know how to use the new power source*. AI is in its 1890s.
- **Palantir as a worked example.** High valuation because of *process engineering*, not pure software. Encoding firm workflows into systems is the moat.
- **Senior leadership adoption gap.** Ironically, the people who *need* AI fluency most — senior leaders — adopt it slowest.

## Key quotes

> *"Individual AI creates chaos... institutional AI creates coordination."*
>
> *"If individual AI cares about saving time, institutional AI cares about scaling revenue."*
>
> *"The future isn't ChatGPT OR domain-specific solutions — it's ChatGPT AND specialized institutional AI working together."*

## How to use this in GWTH

- **Frames the whole gap** between Months 1–2 (individual AI) and Month 3 (institutional AI). This is the pivot lesson of the course.
- **Seven-pillar checklist.** Participants walk through each pillar against their org. Which do they have? Which don't they?
- **The electricity analogy** is a great opener for leadership workshops — it reframes "we're behind" as "we're on time".
