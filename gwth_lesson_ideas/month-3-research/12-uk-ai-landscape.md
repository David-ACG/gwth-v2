# The UK AI Landscape — 2026 Data

*Added 2026-04-20. Source material for UK-specific data points that sit alongside the US/global data in lessons 1–20. Nothing here replaces the McKinsey / PwC / a16z global framing — these are **additional** UK benchmarks so SME leaders can compare themselves to other UK organisations, not just US enterprises.*

## Why UK-specific data matters for GWTH

GWTH participants are majority UK SMEs. The global McKinsey and PwC numbers (20% EBITDA, 20/74 value capture, $3 per $1 ROI) are directionally right but set in US-enterprise context. Students need a UK reference frame to:
- Compare their own progress to UK peers, not just to Fortune 500 outliers.
- Cite UK regulators (FCA, ICO, BoE) in governance conversations at home.
- Point to UK case studies their team can visit, read, or hire from.

## The UK macro picture in 2026

### AI in the UK economy

- **Sector size.** 5,800+ AI companies in the UK contribute £45bn to the economy (2026). (techUK / ibtimes.com.au)
- **GDP potential.** techUK estimates AI could add **£550bn to UK GDP by 2035** — making adoption an urgent economic priority, not a tech curiosity.
- **Mid-sized firm opportunity.** HSBC UK research: AI adoption could unlock **£105bn in additional revenue for UK mid-sized firms by 2030**.
- **Productivity growth in AI-exposed industries.** PwC UK AI Jobs Barometer: productivity growth in AI-exposed industries (financial services, IT, professional services) **nearly quadrupled** — from 7% (2018–2022) to 27% (2018–2024). In least-exposed industries (mining, hospitality) growth *fell* from 10% to 9%.
- **Revenue per employee.** Most AI-exposed industries see **3× higher growth in revenue per employee** than least-exposed.

### UK SME adoption in 2026

- British Chambers of Commerce (March 2026): **54% of UK firms actively using AI**, up from 35% in 2025 and 25% in 2024.
- YouGov UK SME survey: 35% of SMEs using AI, 33% have no plans, 15% planning.
- Only **11% of UK SMEs use AI to a great extent** to automate or streamline — most are still at the "ChatGPT subscription, Copilot licence" surface level without structured workflows.
- **Top adopter sectors**: IT and telecoms (56%), media / marketing / advertising (53%).
- **Laggard sectors**: real estate (11%), transportation (15%), hospitality (18%), manufacturing (19%), retail (19%).
- **Top barriers (UK government AI Adoption Research):** 60% cite limited AI skills and expertise; **71% say they haven't identified a clear use for AI.**

### The UK AI Opportunities Action Plan (Matt Clifford, Jan 2025 / updates 2026)

- 50 recommendations; government has endorsed all 50.
- Core framing: **"AI maker, not just an AI taker."**
- Sovereign compute expansion: **20× by 2030**.
- **AI Growth Zones** announced (first: Culham).
- Adoption approach for public services: **"Scan, Pilot, Scale"** — a three-stage cadence any organisation can borrow.
- Civil service AI package: **Humphrey** — tools named Consult, Parlex, Minute, Lex, Redbox. Targets £80m+ annual savings from consultation analysis alone.
- Research on ~20,000 civil servants using AI: **26 minutes per day per person saved** (= 2 weeks per year), scaling to **1M+ weeks saved annually** across the UK civil service.

### The UK confidence gap (techUK / Public First, 2026)

- Survey of 500 business leaders (tech + wider economy).
- Key finding: a **"confidence gap"** between government AI rhetoric and the operating reality — businesses ready to invest, but want tangible proof the growth agenda is real.
- Top skills gap: **27% of non-tech businesses and 35% of tech businesses** cite expanding AI training programmes as a priority.

### UK financial services — Bank of England view

- BoE chief Andrew Bailey: AI adoption "could shape the UK's economic growth."
- BoE estimate: generative AI could bring **productivity gains of up to 30% to banking, insurance, and capital markets** over 15 years.
- **55% of mid-sized firms** had mainstreamed AI tools (LLMs, analytics, workflow automation) by end of 2025.
- BoE + FCA running **joint AI survey in 2026** to monitor adoption.
- **FCA approach**: will **not introduce AI-specific rules** (Dec 2025 statement from CEO Nikhil Rathi). Five principles apply across regulators: safety, transparency, fairness, accountability, contestability.
- **FCA + ICO** preparing joint guidance on AI and vulnerable customers (early 2026).
