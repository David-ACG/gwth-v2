# AI Trust, Risk & the Right to Deploy AI

**Primary sources:** McKinsey State of AI Trust 2026, McKinsey Agentic AI Governance, Rewired Ch 28 & 34.

## The shift — from "responsible AI" to "right to deploy"

McKinsey's 2026 framing moves the conversation from "we have a responsible AI policy" to "this specific solution has earned the right to ship". It is a *gate*, not a poster on the wall. The shift is driven by agentic AI: when a solution takes autonomous action, the stakes of deployment are materially higher than when it simply answers a question.

## Headline finding — McKinsey AI Trust Maturity Survey 2026

- Average **Responsible AI maturity score: 2.3** (up from 2.0 in 2025).
- Only **~1 in 3** organisations report maturity level 3+ in strategy, governance, or agentic AI governance.
- The 20% of AI leaders (PwC 2026) are **1.7×** more likely to have a Responsible AI framework and **1.5×** more likely to have a cross-functional AI governance board.

## The three failure modes of agentic AI (McKinsey)

Agentic systems don't just get answers wrong — they act. The three concrete failure modes:

1. **Taking unintended actions.** The agent does something outside its charter.
2. **Misusing tools.** The agent uses the right tools, the wrong way.
3. **Operating beyond appropriate guardrails.** The agent exceeds its authority.

## McKinsey's recommended stance

> *"Start with bounded autonomy while keeping humans accountable for high-impact decisions. Scale only when monitoring shows the system behaves predictably."*

Bounded autonomy = small, well-defined decisions. High-impact = anything that touches money, customers, legal obligations. Monitoring = telemetry, not vibes.

## The "right to deploy" gate (Rewired Ch 34)

A concrete set of artefacts a solution must produce *before* it ships:

- Model card (what it is, what it does, what it doesn't).
- Risk register (what could go wrong, who owns each risk).
- Fairness review (who could be disadvantaged).
- Monitoring plan (how we'll know if something breaks).
- Escalation path (who takes over when something breaks).
- Kill switch (how we stop it).

None of this is new as a list. What is new is the idea that the gate is *opt-out, not opt-in* — you don't ship until you've earned it.

## Trust as growth engine, not brake

The 2026 empirical finding (PwC, Forrester, McKinsey alike): the orgs with the strongest governance are also the ones deploying AI most aggressively and most autonomously. Governance doesn't slow you down; *lack of* governance does (because you can't safely scale).

> *"AI trust is the new growth engine."* — MarTech 2026.

## The PURE framework (DBS — from case study)

A four-word customer-facing operational summary of responsible AI:
- **P**urposeful
- **U**nsurprising
- **R**espectful
- **E**xplainable

Adaptable for any SME as a short-form internal policy.

## How to use this in GWTH

- **Risk & governance week of Month 3.** Participants build their own "right to deploy" one-pager for an AI solution they have or are planning.
- **Debunks "governance = slow".** The data says otherwise. This is a permission-giving lesson for SME leaders who think governance is a thing big companies do to themselves.
- **PURE-style takeaway.** Each participant writes their own four-word operational test for AI use on customer data.
- **Sets up the Month 3 closing argument.** Training the team (via GWTH.ai or otherwise) is what lets governance scale without a bottleneck.
