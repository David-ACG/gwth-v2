## Edition changes at a glance

Authors: **Lamarre, Smaje, Levin, Singla, Sukharevsky** (replacing 1st-ed author Rodney Zemmel; adding QuantumBlack leaders Singla and Sukharevsky). 39 chapters across seven sections, with the following noted per the Wiley TOC:

- **NEW** chapters: 2, 5, 8, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 20, 21, 22, 23, 28, 29, 33, 35, 36, 37.
- **EXPANDED** chapters: 1, 3, 4, 7, 9, 12, 19, 24, 25, 30, 31, 34, 38, 39.
- **Unchanged** (no label): 6, 10, 18, 26, 27, 32.
- Case studies: **LATAM Airlines, Toyota** (new); **Freeport-McMoRan, DBS Bank** (expanded). LEGO case from 1st ed is dropped.
- Section 3 renamed to "An Operating Model That Outruns the Competition".
- Section 6 renamed to "Adoption and Scaling: Your Force Multipliers".
- Introduction now contains a "Your transformation manifesto" section at p. 12.

## 2nd-edition-specific themes to track through highlights

Drawn from the Wiley description and the NEW/EXPANDED chapter labels:

- **The economics of Tech & AI transformations** (Ch 2, new) — likely the most-cited analytical framing in the book; flag any new unit economics, payback curves, or gen-AI value-at-stake models.
- **Agentic AI** — runs across Ch 5 (roadmap), Ch 11 (talent), Ch 22 (building agents). Treat as a spine, not a chapter.
- **Tech muscle of business leaders** (Ch 8, new) — the "business leader capability upgrade" thread that was largely absent from the 1st edition.
- **AI-assisted software development** (Ch 20, new) — likely the most concrete developer-productivity material in the book.
- **Proprietary data advantage** (Ch 29, new) — a strategic-differentiation framing that upgrades the 1st edition's data-products treatment.
- **Midstream adjustments** (Ch 33, new) — the realism chapter; likely a good source of candid "what goes wrong" material for the GWTH course.
- **"Right to deploy AI"** (Ch 34, expanded) — governance and risk framing updated for the agentic era.
