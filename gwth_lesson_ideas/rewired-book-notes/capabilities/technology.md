# Technology

The 2nd-edition technology section is **substantially rewritten**. Five of the seven chapters are new. Four of them are explicitly AI-centric: Ch 20 (AI-assisted software development), Ch 21 (AI platform for model development), Ch 22 (building agentic AI solutions), Ch 23 (core-system modernisation). The 1st edition's "decoupled architecture / cloud / engineering practices / developer tools / production-grade / security" skeleton has been compressed into fewer chapters (Ch 17–19) and flanked by AI-specific craft chapters.

## Core framework (2nd edition)

1. **Platforms that power the enterprise** (Ch 17, new) — the enterprise-platform layer; likely covers internal developer platforms, data platforms, AI platforms as siblings.
2. **APIs: flexible and scalable** (Ch 18) — the decoupling backbone; carry-over from the 1st edition.
3. **Engineering practices for speed and quality** (Ch 19, expanded) — CI/CD, testing, code review, trunk-based development — updated for the AI era.
4. **AI revolution in software development** (Ch 20, new) — AI coding assistants, agent-assisted reviews, AI-native CI/CD.
5. **AI platform to accelerate model development** (Ch 21, new) — the infrastructure layer for training, evaluating, serving models.
6. **Developing agentic AI solutions** (Ch 22, new) — the craft of building agents: design patterns, tool use, evaluation, guardrails.
7. **Surgical approach to modernising core systems** (Ch 23, new) — the legacy-estate chapter; focused, value-backed modernisation rather than lift-and-shift.

## 2nd-edition-specific watchouts

- **Ch 20** is probably the most quotable chapter for any audience interested in developer productivity uplift. Look for adoption data, productivity deltas, and the authors' take on where AI coding tools are and aren't ready.
- **Ch 22** is the most opinionated chapter in this section — it commits to specific patterns for agent construction. Highlight definitional passages (what counts as an agentic AI solution in McKinsey's framing) and any decision criteria for "agent vs workflow".
- **Ch 23** (core modernisation) is notable for what it *doesn't* say — read for implicit claims about legacy ERP, mainframe, or ageing bespoke systems. Flag anything that materially changes the investment thesis for modernisation.
- The 1st edition's "MLOps" chapter appears to have moved — content likely split across Ch 21 (AI platform) and Ch 34 (risk/deployment).

## GWTH course application

- Ch 20 is GWTH-relevant because many course participants will already be experimenting with AI coding tools. Use the chapter for credibility and evidence; caveat all specific tool claims.
- Ch 22 is advanced material — best used as a second-tier module for participants building their own agentic solutions.
- Skip the platform/API content (Ch 17, Ch 18) for the main GWTH track; keep for the optional technology-leader track.
