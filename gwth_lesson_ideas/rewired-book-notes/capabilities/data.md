# Data

The 2nd edition keeps the section's title but rewires its narrative for the LLM/agentic era. The opening shifts to a value-first frame (Ch 24, expanded: "Start with the value, then get the data"). Two genuinely new chapters anchor the back half: **Protecting data in an LLM world** (Ch 28) and **Create a proprietary data advantage** (Ch 29). Data products (Ch 26) and data organisation (Ch 27) carry over from the 1st edition.

## Core framework (2nd edition)

1. **Start with the value, then get the data** (Ch 24, expanded) — value-first prioritisation; identify what data matters, build a roadmap. Replaces the 1st-edition's "MLOps so AI can scale" opener.
2. **No data architecture, no AI advantage** (Ch 25, expanded) — the architecture argument; AI is bottlenecked by data plumbing.
3. **Data products: reusable building blocks for scaling** (Ch 26) — packaged, owned, consumable data; built by data-product pods.
4. **Organise to get the most from your data** (Ch 27) — roles, ownership, funding, governance.
5. **Protecting data in an LLM world** (Ch 28, new) — privacy, IP, leakage, prompt injection, model-training risks. The 2nd edition's data-security chapter.
6. **Your next strategic duty: create a proprietary data advantage** (Ch 29, new) — data as competitive moat in a world where models are commoditised. Strategic-differentiation framing.

## 2nd-edition-specific watchouts

- **Ch 28** (LLM data protection) is one of the highest-stakes chapters for any company actively deploying LLMs. Look for concrete patterns: data-classification schemes, vendor controls, training-vs-inference separation. Highlight aggressively.
- **Ch 29** (proprietary data advantage) reframes data strategy in the gen-AI era — when foundation models are accessible to everyone, your data becomes the differentiator. Look for guidance on which data is genuinely proprietary, defensible and useful.
- **MLOps content** has likely moved out of this section in the 2nd edition — check Ch 21 (AI platform for model development) and Ch 34 (risk/deployment) for the equivalent material.
- The data-products construct (Ch 26) carries forward, but verify whether it now extends to **knowledge bases / vector stores / RAG corpuses** as a recognised data-product type.

## GWTH course application

- Ch 29 is the standout chapter for a GWTH strategic-positioning module: "what is your proprietary data, and how do you build more of it?" is a tractable strategic question for participants.
- Ch 28 is essential for any GWTH participant deploying gen AI on customer or sensitive data — likely deserves its own short module.
- Ch 26's data-products framing remains useful for SMEs as a way to think about treating internal data sets the way Amazon treats internal products.
