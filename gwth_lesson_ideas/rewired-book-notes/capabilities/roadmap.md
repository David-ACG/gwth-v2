# Roadmap

The transformation roadmap is, in *Rewired*'s framing, a **business-led** plan — not a technology plan — that sequences the digital and AI solutions a business will build against the KPIs they are meant to move. It binds the C-suite to a phased, value-backed programme of domain-by-domain work. The 2nd edition re-scopes this section from the 1st edition: "use cases" are explicitly **demoted** in favour of domains (Ch 3, expanded) and a new chapter on **the economics of Tech & AI transformations** (Ch 2, new) installs a financial and value-creation frame before the domain work begins.

## Core framework

Section One proceeds in seven steps, each roughly a chapter:

1. **Top team alignment** (Ch 1, expanded) — shared vision, scope, value aspiration; senior leaders invest in learning what's possible.
2. **Economics of Tech & AI transformations** (Ch 2, new) — the financial frame: unit economics, payback, value-at-stake. Key 2nd-edition addition.
3. **Domains, not use cases** (Ch 3, expanded) — the unit of transformation is a business domain (a journey, process, or product line). Use-case-hunting is an anti-pattern.
4. **Business leaders lead the reimagination** (Ch 4, expanded) — functional owners own the redesign of their domain, not central IT.
5. **Reimagining workflows with agentic AI** (Ch 5, new) — a sibling chapter to Ch 4, focused on what agents change about how work flows through a domain.
6. **Capabilities you build** (Ch 6) — the enterprise capabilities (talent, op model, tech, data, adoption) needed to deliver.
7. **Corporate team sport** (Ch 7, expanded) — the multi-function, multi-leader participation required for any of this to stick.

## 2nd-edition-specific watchouts

- **Ch 2** (new): the economics chapter is likely the single most citable analytical framing in the book. Highlight any unit-economics models, new-economics frames, gen-AI payback curves, or "new value function" language.
- **Ch 5** (new): agentic workflow reimagination. Expect fewer "pick your AI use case" heuristics and more "what does the end-to-end process look like if the work is done by a human–agent team".
- The 2nd edition explicitly mentions **"building the tech muscle of AI transformation leaders"** on the Wiley jacket — this is Ch 8 in the Talent section, but it's motivated by Roadmap-section material; cross-ref once verified.

## GWTH course application

- The economics chapter (Ch 2) deserves its own GWTH module — small businesses in particular need a credible way to size AI investment against expected return, and McKinsey's framing is a serviceable starting point.
- The domains-not-use-cases argument (Ch 3) is the opening diagnostic question for any GWTH participant: "What domain are you transforming?" not "What AI use case are you trying?"
- The agentic-workflow chapter (Ch 5) is the most forward-looking content in Section One; use with care — the examples may date quickly.
