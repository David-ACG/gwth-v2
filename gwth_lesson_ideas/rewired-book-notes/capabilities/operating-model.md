# Operating model

The 2nd edition renames this section from "A New Operating Model" to **"An Operating Model That Outruns the Competition"** — the framing shifts from "new" to "fast". Four of the five chapters (13, 14, 15, 16) are new. The 1st edition's agile-heavy content appears to have been consolidated or replaced with a sharper narrative on (a) **embedding engineering in the business** (Ch 13), (b) **a blueprint for enterprise speed** (Ch 14), (c) **controlled transition** (Ch 15), and (d) **new-business operating models** (Ch 16).

## Core framework (2nd edition)

1. **Go team! The ground floor** (Ch 12, expanded) — the foundational team unit (likely still a pod or cross-functional squad) and how it sits inside the wider enterprise.
2. **Embedding engineering talent in the business** (Ch 13, new) — the "revolutionary idea": move engineers out of IT and into the business domains that own outcomes.
3. **Blueprint for a faster enterprise** (Ch 14, new) — the enterprise-scale structure, governance and funding model that supports speed.
4. **Making the transition in a controlled way** (Ch 15, new) — practical sequencing for an organisation migrating to the new model.
5. **An operating model for building a new business** (Ch 16, new) — greenfield / "build-a-new-business" framing; distinct from transforming an existing P&L.

## Note on 1st-edition content

The 1st edition's three canonical operating models — **digital factory, product and platform, enterprise-wide agile** — are likely still referenced (McKinsey has used them consistently across articles) but may no longer be the backbone of the section. Verify whether Ch 14 ("blueprint") explicitly enumerates them.

## 2nd-edition-specific watchouts

- **Embedding engineering** (Ch 13): a notable reversal of the 1st-edition "digital factory" emphasis. If the 1st edition argued "centralise then diffuse", the 2nd may argue "distribute early". Highlight for the GWTH course — it materially changes advice for medium-sized organisations.
- **Controlled transition** (Ch 15): look for phasing language, risk gates, pilot-to-scale heuristics.
- **Agentic AI operating-model impact** — likely woven through Ch 12 (team ground floor) and Ch 22 (building agentic solutions). Not a dedicated chapter.

## GWTH course application

- Ch 13 is one of the highest-value chapters for a GWTH audience: it argues against the "let IT handle AI" default that many mid-sized businesses drift into.
- Ch 16 ("building a new business") is a natural fit for a GWTH module on AI-native venturing — participants who are starting something new rather than transforming something old.
- Ch 12's "ground floor" framing is a clean entry point for a team-design module — use as the conceptual anchor before any specific pod/tribe terminology.
