## The expanded 2025–2026 benchmark landscape

### 1. **Section AI Proficiency Report (2024 → mid-2025)** — *the key new source*
**Sample:** 5,003 knowledge workers across US, UK, Canada (later wave: 5,013)
**Method:** Three-part empirical assessment — AI Usage (self-report), AI Knowledge (test), Prompting Capability (AI-scored prompting simulations). Each participant given an overall score and tier.

**The five-tier breakdown:**
| Tier | % of workforce | Profile |
|------|----------------|---------|
| **AI Experts** | **1%** | Daily paid-tool use, 80%+ prompting score, 8–12 hours/week saved, 100% trust AI output, multi-purpose use |
| **AI Practitioners** | 8% | Weekly paid/free tools, ~10% time saved, moderate prompting, supportive employers |
| **AI Experimenters** | 34% | Tinkering, low integration, minimal time savings |
| **AI Novices** | 47% | Sparse use, basic only |
| **AI Skeptics** | 11% | Active avoidance |

**Subsequent waves (mid-2025):** Only 10% of workforce scores as "AI-proficient" (Experts + Practitioners combined). Less than 3% are AI practitioners or experts in newer measurement.

**Why this is your strongest source:**
- It is the **only major public study that uses an empirical assessment** rather than self-report
- It explicitly identifies a 1% tier with measurable criteria
- It includes the UK (alongside US and Canada)
- The methodology — usage + knowledge + prompting scored — is directly **portable to GWTH's own assessment**

**Sources:**
- Section AI Proficiency Report (2024): https://www.sectionai.com/ai/ai-proficiency-report
- Section "AI Proficiency Crisis" update (June 2025): https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250610142741/en/

**How GWTH uses it:** *"GWTH Level 5 is calibrated to Section's 'AI Expert' tier — the top 1% of knowledge workers identified in a 5,000-person US/UK/Canada study. Like Section's Experts, Level 5 graduates demonstrate daily multi-tool use, 80%+ prompting capability, and material weekly productivity gains."*

This is the claim that gives you the literal "top 1%" with citable evidence.

## Why combining sources is defensible (not cherry-picking)

The ASA requires substantiation, not single-source proof. Combining sources is **stronger** than relying on one, provided:

1. **Each source measures the same construct** (advanced/expert AI use in knowledge work) ✓
2. **Methodologies are disclosed** (Section: empirical assessment; EY: self-report + employer cross-check; Microsoft: survey + telemetry) ✓
3. **Results triangulate rather than contradict** — they do: 1%, 5%, 8–16% in clear hierarchy ✓
4. **Geographic coverage is appropriate** — Section and Microsoft both include UK explicitly; EY is global with UK subset ✓

The triangulation argument is actually **stronger marketing** than any single statistic because it shows you've done the homework. "Three independent studies of 40,000+ workers" beats "one study of 5,000".

## Using US data legitimately for a UK product

The US data isn't a workaround — it's a strength, if framed correctly:

**Good framing:**
- "Across US, UK, and Canadian knowledge workers (Section 2025)..."
- "In a 10-market study including the UK (Microsoft WTI 2026)..."
- "Global benchmark of 15,000 employees with UK subset (EY 2025)..."

**Avoid:**
- "US workers" cited as if equivalent to UK
- Single-country US-only data presented for UK audiences

The Section study **is** UK-inclusive (US + UK + Canada combined sample). This is the most important fact for your defensibility — your headline 1% claim has a UK component built in.

## Recommended assessment architecture (now empirically aligned)

To **earn** the top-1% claim, GWTH's Level 5 assessment should mirror Section's three-part method:

### A. Usage assessment (20% of score)
- Daily multi-tool use (Claude, ChatGPT, sector-specific tools)
- Paid-tier subscriptions evidenced
- Multiple use cases across functions
- *Section threshold: 100% of Experts use paid tools several times weekly*

### B. Knowledge assessment (30% of score)
- AI capabilities and limitations
- Responsible use, risk awareness
- Model differences and selection
- *Section threshold: tested via standardised questions*

### C. Prompting capability (50% of score)
- AI-scored prompt simulations across multiple scenarios
- Quality control / output evaluation
- Mode-switching (Microsoft's asking/delegation/collaboration/exploration)
- *Section threshold: Experts averaged 80%+ on prompting assessment*

This architecture means you can publish: *"GWTH Level 5 uses the same three-dimensional empirical assessment framework as Section's 2025 AI Proficiency Report, which identified the top 1% of knowledge workers."*

That's the line that closes the regulatory and credibility loop.

## Phase 2: build your own data — a two-stage approach

Phase 2 should run on **two parallel tracks**: an applicant questionnaire (free, immediate) and a commissioned UK baseline study (paid, 6–12 months in). They are not interchangeable — each unlocks a different class of claim.

### Stage 2A: Pre-signup applicant questionnaire (start immediately)

A short questionnaire taken before signup, returning a personalised GWTH Score (e.g. *"You're currently Level 2.3"*).

**What this gives you that's genuinely powerful:**

1. **Personalisation and pricing levers** — score-based routing to the right starting level; potential for differentiated pricing (Level 1 starters at full £29/month; higher entrants on accelerated tracks).
2. **Conversion mechanic** — *"You're Level 2. Here's what it takes to reach Level 5"* is a far stickier lead-capture than a generic signup form. Outskill and GrowthSchool don't do this.
3. **Before/after evidence** — match entry scores to exit scores for the same student. *"GWTH students improve an average of 2.3 levels in 12 weeks"* is bulletproof marketing: internal data, individual-level change, no baseline-comparison problem.
4. **Cohort intelligence** — after 500 applicants you know which sectors are over/under-represented, where people struggle, where to invest course development.
5. **Effectively free data collection** — Section paid for 5,000 respondents; you get scored applicants as a side-effect of signup.

**Critical caveat — what this is NOT:**

This is **not** a UK baseline study. Your sample is **self-selected** (people interested in AI education), so it skews systematically higher than UK average: more digitally engaged, more AI-curious, probably younger and more educated. You cannot use applicant data to claim "only X% of UK adults score at Level 5" — selection bias invalidates it.

**Claims you CAN make from applicant data:**
- *"95% of GWTH applicants enter below Level 3 — by Level 5, they're in the top 1% of knowledge workers (Section 2025)."*
- *"GWTH applicants enter at an average AI proficiency of [X], well above the Ofcom 2026 UK baseline of 54% any-use."*
- *"Across [N] GWTH applicants, the distribution of incoming AI skills shows..."*

**Claims you CANNOT make from applicant data:**
- *"We surveyed [N] UK adults and found..."* — your sample isn't representative.
- *"Only X% of UK adults score at Level 5"* based on your own data alone.

**Design notes for the questionnaire:**
- **Length:** 5–7 minutes, 15–20 questions. Drop-off rises sharply beyond this.
- **Structure:** Mirror Section's three dimensions in miniature — usage self-report, knowledge MCQs, *one or two* short prompting tasks (full prompting simulations belong in the Level 5 certification test, not the entry triage).
- **Output:** A score + level + a personalised "what you'll learn to do next" page. The level result is what makes people share it on LinkedIn.
- **Storage:** Keep raw responses, not just scores. You'll want to re-score later if the framework evolves.
- **Repeatability:** Let applicants retake post-course. The delta is the marketing.

**Don't try to certify Level 5 at the front door.** The entry questionnaire is a triage and lead-capture tool. The full empirical assessment (Section-style) is the post-course certification instrument.

### Stage 2B: Commissioned UK baseline study (6–12 months in)

Once GWTH has revenue and the applicant questionnaire is running, commission a **separate** UK-representative sample taking the same assessment.

- **Provider:** YouGov or Prolific
- **Sample:** 1,500–2,000 UK adults, weighted to ONS demographics (age, region, education, employment)
- **Cost:** £8–15k
- **Output:** A measured UK distribution → *"In our December 2026 UK benchmark of 1,500 adults weighted to ONS demographics, only 1.1% met the GWTH Level 5 standard."*

This is the study that lets you make percentile claims about the UK population — which the applicant questionnaire alone never can.

**Why both, not just one:**

| | Applicant questionnaire | Commissioned baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free (build once) | £8–15k |
| Speed | Live from launch | 4–6 weeks to field |
| Sample | Self-selected, biased high | UK-representative |
| Funnel personalisation | ✓ | ✗ |
| Before/after evidence | ✓ | ✗ |
| Cohort intelligence | ✓ | Limited |
| UK percentile claims | ✗ | ✓ |
| PR / white paper asset | Weaker | Strong |
| ASA-defensible "% of UK adults" | ✗ | ✓ |

They share the same assessment infrastructure — you build the test once and deploy it twice. The applicant questionnaire fills the funnel; the commissioned baseline anchors the headline claim.

### Phase 2 reasons to do this (combined)

1. **You own the datasets** — Section, Microsoft and EY don't share theirs.
2. **Annual updates** keep claims current as AI proficiency rises.
3. **UK-only sample** removes the "but those are US numbers" objection entirely.
4. **PR / white paper value** — *"GWTH AI Literacy Report 2026"* becomes an annual lead-gen asset that competitors can't match.
5. **Method already designed** — replicate Section's three-part assessment.
6. **Funnel intelligence + market positioning in one infrastructure build.**

By late 2026 / early 2027, the stacked-source claim from Phase 1 gets replaced with: *"GWTH-measured: in our December 2026 UK benchmark of 1,500 adults weighted to ONS demographics, only 1.1% met the GWTH Level 5 standard, while 96% of our graduates do."*

## Risk register

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Section data uses self-report for usage | Two of three Section measures are tested (knowledge, prompting). Usage is a small share of overall score. |
| US-heavy samples | Section explicitly UK-inclusive; Microsoft includes UK; EY has UK subset. Frame as "international" not "US". |
| AI literacy rising fast (1% today → 5% in 18 months) | Annual claim review; "as of 2025/2026" framing; Phase 2 own benchmark |
| Ceiling effects in GWTH Level 5 (too many pass) | Pilot with three cohorts before launch (see v1) |
| **Selection bias in applicant questionnaire data** | **Never present applicant data as a UK baseline. Use only for funnel claims, before/after evidence, and cohort intelligence. UK percentile claims come from Stage 2B only.** |
| **Front-door questionnaire drop-off** | **Keep to 5–7 minutes / 15–20 questions; one or two prompting tasks max; full Section-style assessment reserved for post-course certification.** |
| ASA challenge | Methodology page on site within two clicks of any claim, named sources, conservative language |
